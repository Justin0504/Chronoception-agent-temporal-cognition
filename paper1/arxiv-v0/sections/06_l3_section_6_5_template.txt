\subsection{A positive control: wall-clock SFT crosses $\ccestar$ on T3.1}
\label{ssec:a1-positive-control}

CIT (Theorem~\ref{thm:cit}) is a negative result: it says that token-only training cannot install chronoception. The natural question is whether a training procedure that \emph{does} include wall-clock signal can install it. We provide a toy positive control. The construction is deliberately minimal: it tests the existence of the converse direction, not its full realisation (which is the program of \textsc{ChronoStack}, Paper 2).

\paragraph{Construction.}
We take Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct as the base model and generate {\itshape{\bfseries 500}}\,SFT pairs by running the base model on T3.1 prompts, measuring each generation's actual $\twall$, and constructing a training target of the form ``\textit{\{response\}. This task took approximately \{$\twall$\}\,seconds}'' with $\pm 15\%$ Gaussian noise on the duration to mimic a calibrated human-style estimate. We LoRA fine-tune the base model on these pairs (rank $16$, three epochs). Concretely, the SFT loss is token-level cross-entropy as before, but the training data carries wall-clock signal in the targets, so the support of the loss includes wall-clock duration. The construction exits the CIT regime by extending the support of $\mathcal{D}$ rather than by changing the form of $\ell$.

\paragraph{What the positive control demonstrates.}
The construction is a toy --- it addresses only T3.1, only one model, and uses synthetic durations rather than a richer wall-clock-supported loss. It is sufficient nonetheless to demonstrate the converse direction of CIT: \textbf{when wall-clock signal is in the loss support, narrative-axis chronoception is installable.} The full installation that closes $\cce$ across all nine sub-capabilities --- including action-axis L2, which a target-side annotation alone cannot reach --- is the program of \textsc{ChronoStack} (Paper 2). The toy control is the first existence proof in the framework's own measurement system, and answers the obvious objection that the framework's central claim is purely negative.
