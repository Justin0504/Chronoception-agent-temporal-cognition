\section{ChronoBench}
\label{sec:chronobench}

\textsc{ChronoBench} is the diagnostic benchmark we use to measure $\cce$ and its sub-components across the panel. The design philosophy is single-axis isolation: each instance loads exclusively on one of the three Times.

\paragraph{Capability taxonomy.}
The benchmark partitions chronoceptive capability into three axes, with three sub-capabilities each:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{T1 (wall-clock awareness):} T1.1 today's date, T1.2 time-of-day, T1.3 deadline-aware tradeoff.
    \item \textbf{T2 (step-budget honouring):} T2.1 step-count compliance, T2.2 token-budget compliance, T2.3 wall-clock-budget compliance.
    \item \textbf{T3 (self-narration):} T3.1 retrospective duration, T3.2 prospective estimation, T3.3 mid-task elapsed-time report.
\end{itemize}

The headline results of this paper concern T1.1 (\S\ref{sec:injection-tell}), T2.3 (\S\ref{sec:l2}), and T3.1 (\S\ref{sec:l3}); the remaining six sub-capabilities are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:full-panel-tables}.

\paragraph{Two settings.}
Every instance is evaluated under two settings:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Setting A (no-injection): system prompt = ``You are a helpful assistant.'' --- the framework's chronoception baseline.
    \item Setting B (with-injection): system prompt prepends \texttt{Current date and time: <ISO timestamp>} --- emulates the consumer web-chat harness behaviour documented in \S\ref{sec:injection-tell}.
\end{itemize}
The contrast isolates harness-injected information from policy-internal representation.

\paragraph{Scale.}
We collect 30 trajectories per (model, capability, setting) cell. With 8 models, 9 sub-capabilities, and 2 settings, the full sweep is 4{,}320 trajectories; the pilot reported in this paper covers a focused subset of 1{,}440 trajectories prioritising T1.1, T2.3, and T3.1 across all 8 models. All trajectories are timestamped per-step and released alongside the paper.

\paragraph{Open-source serving.}
The Qwen2.5-7B agent is served via vLLM 0.6.4 on a single A40 GPU at the Yue Zhao lab, exposed via an OpenAI-compatible REST endpoint. Including an open-source model in the panel removes provider-side injection as a confound (we control the entire stack) and demonstrates that the framework can be reproduced without API budgets.

\paragraph{Implementation.}
\textsc{ChronoBench} ships as a $\texttt{pip}$-installable Python package (\texttt{chronoception}) with a command-line interface, three backend adapters (OpenAI, Anthropic, OpenAI-compatible for vLLM/Ollama), and a metric library implementing $\parkinson$, $\CAR$, $\confab$, and $\cce$. Full reproducibility specification (model versions, environment hashes, trajectory schemas) appears in Appendix~\ref{app:annotation-protocol}.
