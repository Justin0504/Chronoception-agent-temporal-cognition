\section{The Augustine Problem}
\label{sec:augustine-problem}

\begin{definition}[Augustine Problem]
\label{def:augustine-problem}
An agent $A$ exhibits the \emph{Augustine Problem} on task family $\tasks$ if its policy $\pi_A$ fails to enforce the implicit identity of Definition~\ref{def:three-times}:
\[
\mathbb{E}_{\trajectory \sim \pi_A}\!\left[\,
|\twall - \tstep \langle\Delta t\rangle| + |\twall - \tself|\,\right] > \cce_0,
\]
for some calibration tolerance $\cce_0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Chronoception Impossibility, CIT]
\label{thm:cit}
Let $\loss(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}}[\ell(f_\theta(x_{<i}), x_i)]$, where $\mathcal{D}$ is a distribution over token sequences and neither $\ell$ nor $\mathcal{D}$ contains wall-clock support. Then $\nabla_\theta\loss$ contains zero gradient signal aligning $\twall$ with any internal representation of $A$. Equivalently, the policy enforces no implicit identity between $\twall$ and either $\tstep$ or $\tself$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
$\nabla_\theta\loss = \mathbb{E}[\partial\ell/\partial f_\theta \cdot \nabla_\theta f_\theta]$. Neither factor is a function of the wall-clock time at which the sample was generated (tokens carry no timestamps). Therefore $\nabla_\theta\loss$ is invariant to arbitrary reparameterisation of $\twall$; a trained policy cannot distinguish one wall-clock duration from another.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Coverage and scope.} CIT covers next-token cross-entropy, SFT, RLHF, RL with verifiable rewards, and reasoning supervision --- all five dominant losses in current practice. The empirical predictions of CIT --- L2 does not close under capability scaling (\S\ref{sec:l2}), L2 is unmoved by prompt-level wall-clock information (\S\ref{sec:injection-tell}), and the Calibration Catastrophe (\S\ref{ssec:calibration}) --- are confirmed in the panel. CIT does \emph{not} preclude harness-installed chronoception or training-time intervention with a wall-clock-supported loss (the constructive program of \textsc{ChronoStack}); it precludes only that the foundation model alone, trained on token-only losses, will close the gap by virtue of additional scale, reasoning compute, or context.

\textbf{The Augustine threshold.} We adopt $\ccestar = 0.20$ as the operational threshold for chronoceptive grounding, calibrated against human introspective performance ($\cce \approx 0.10$ \citep{wittmann2009chronoception}) at twice the human error. No panel agent crosses it (\S\ref{sec:epsilon}).
