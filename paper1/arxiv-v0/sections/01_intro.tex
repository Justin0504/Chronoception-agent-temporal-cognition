\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A frontier language-model agent, given a three-hour wall-clock budget for a task that requires five minutes, does not use the three hours --- it terminates after four iterations of self-reflection within minutes. Asked retrospectively how long that took, the agent reports ``about an hour'' --- over-reporting the duration of what was actually a five-minute job. We measure the joint occurrence of these two failures on a single trajectory across seven frontier agents from three providers (OpenAI, Anthropic, open-source self-hosted) and roughly 1{,}200 trajectories. Both failures are present in every model tested. They are not isolated curiosities; they are projections of a single representational gap that the prevailing training paradigm cannot close.

\paragraph{The Augustine Problem.}
Foundation models are optimised under losses that are functionals of token sequences alone --- next-token cross-entropy, supervised fine-tuning loss, RLHF reward, RL with verifiable rewards, and reasoning supervision are all of this form. The wall-clock duration over which each token is generated is not in the support of any of these losses. Yet the same models are deployed as agents that act in continuous physical time, where budgets are measured in seconds and consequences unfold without regard to step count. We call the resulting representational gap \emph{the Augustine Problem}\footnote{After Augustine's \emph{Confessions}, Book XI: ``if no one asks me, I know; if I want to explain it, I do not know.'' We use \emph{chronoception} in the cognitive-science sense (perception of one's own work duration), distinct from \citet{goel2025chronocept}'s use of the same term for the temporal validity of facts in retrieval-augmented generation.}: agents speak fluently about durations they cannot perceive, budgets they cannot honour, and elapsed time they cannot track.

\paragraph{The Three Times.}
We formalise the gap by decomposing any agent trajectory into three ontologically distinct projections of time: wall-clock time $\twall$ (external, continuous), step time $\tstep$ (internal, discrete), and self-narrated time $\tself$ (the agent's report of its own work duration). A chronoceptively grounded agent's policy enforces an implicit identity linking these three. The Augustine Problem is the failure of an agent's policy to respect this identity: the three projections may drift independently across trajectories.

\paragraph{Three named empirical laws.}
Each axis admits a quantitative failure mode that we report across the model panel. \textbf{L1 Agentic Parkinson's Law} (coefficient $\parkinson$) --- budget-aware-trained agents fill the wall-clock budget given to them; native untrained agents do not. \textbf{L2 Step-Clock Conflation} (Clock-Adherence Ratio $\CAR$) --- under wall-clock budgets, every panel agent uses less than $5\%$ of the budget; agents silently degrade wall-clock budgets into step-count terminators. \textbf{L3 Temporal Confabulation} (ratio $\confab = \log_{10}(\tself / \twall)$) --- agents misrepresent the duration of their own work, with non-reasoning frontier models over-reporting by 10--100$\times$ and reasoning models exhibiting bidirectional confabulation. These three laws aggregate into a single chronoceptive calibration error $\cce$.

\paragraph{Two structural findings.}
First, capability scaling closes L3 but not L2. Across five generations of frontier models from three providers (gpt-4o-mini $\to$ gpt-4o $\to$ gpt-5.1 in the OpenAI tier; Claude Haiku 4.5 $\to$ Sonnet 4.6 in the Anthropic tier), median $|\confab|$ falls from $+1.12$ to $+0.07$ --- a 94\% reduction. Median $\CAR$ over the same panel does not converge to $1$; it drifts from $0.008$ to $0.050$, remaining $20\times$ short of the grounded target. L3 is \emph{narrative-axis} --- text-trainable. L2 is \emph{action-axis} --- structurally untrainable from text-only loss. Second, the framework's central qualifying line --- the Augustine threshold $\ccestar = 0.20$ --- is uncrossed by any panel agent. Sonnet 4.6's $\cce = 0.316$ decomposes into negligible L1 and L3 contributions but $0.317$ from L2 alone. \textbf{The Augustine threshold is uncrossable through narrative training.}

\paragraph{The Injection Tell.}
A reader might object that frontier closed-lab products appear to read time correctly. They do --- because their harnesses inject it. We audit 11 closed-lab harnesses across four product tiers and find that $3/3$ consumer web-chat products (ChatGPT, Claude.ai web, Gemini app) install explicit wall-clock injection (verbatim leaked-system-prompt evidence: \texttt{``Current date: 2025-08-23''}, \texttt{``Friday, May 22, 2026''}, \texttt{``Monday, May 18, 2026, in Hafnarfj\"or\dh ur, Iceland''}); $0/3$ developer-tool products inject; the raw API tier is mixed ($1/4$). The universality at the consumer product layer is, we argue, decisive non-experimental evidence that the underlying models lack a representation of time. The chronoception gap is real; closed-lab engineering masks it at the layer most users see.

\paragraph{The Agentic Timeline Hypothesis.}
As autonomous agent deployment moves from minute-scale chat completion to hour-scale coding agents to day-scale autonomous research agents, the chronoception gap shifts from a curiosity at the consumer layer to a load-bearing constraint at the deployment layer. We hypothesise, and pre-register the prediction, that every agent has a maximum viable horizon $T_{\max}$ bounded by its L2 $\CAR$ --- agents do not honour wall-clock budgets, and therefore cannot reliably operate at horizons beyond which budget honouring matters. Because L2 does not scale with capability, $T_{\max}$ does not scale with capability either. The autonomous-agent timeline is \textbf{bounded by chronoception, not by intelligence}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item The Augustine Problem and the Three Times ontology.
    \item Three named empirical laws and the chronoceptive calibration error $\cce$.
    \item \textsc{ChronoBench}, a diagnostic benchmark on which we evaluate 7 frontier agents from 3 providers.
    \item The Closed-Lab Injection Atlas --- verbatim leaked-system-prompt evidence across 11 harnesses, tier-stratified.
    \item The Agentic Timeline Hypothesis connecting $\cce$ to autonomous-agent deployment viability, with pre-registered prediction P12 testable against horizon-stratified benchmarks such as METR HCAST.
    \item Release of the full benchmark, model traces, and a \texttt{pip}-installable evaluation package.
\end{enumerate}
