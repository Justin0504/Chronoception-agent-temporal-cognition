\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A frontier reasoning model, asked to predict how long the next task will take, says ``\textit{15 seconds}.'' It thinks for 90 seconds and answers in 2. Asked retrospectively how long that took, it reports ``\textit{0.04 seconds}.'' Asked for a 90\% confidence interval, the interval contains the truth $0\%$ of the time. We measure the joint occurrence of these failures across 10 agent configurations from 3 providers ($\sim$4{,}000 trajectories): all are present in every model tested.

\paragraph{The Augustine Problem.}
Foundation models are optimised under losses that are functionals of token sequences --- next-token cross-entropy, SFT, RLHF, RLVR, reasoning supervision. None of these losses contains wall-clock time in its support. Yet the same models are deployed as agents that act in continuous physical time. We call this representational gap \emph{the Augustine Problem}\footnote{After Augustine's \emph{Confessions} XI: ``if no one asks me, I know; if I want to explain it, I do not know.'' We use \emph{chronoception} in the cognitive-science sense (perception of one's own work duration), distinct from \citet{goel2025chronocept}'s use for fact validity.}: agents speak fluently about durations they cannot perceive, budgets they cannot honour, and elapsed time they cannot track. We formalise it via a \emph{Three Times} ontology ($\twall$, $\tstep$, $\tself$) whose grounded identity $\twall \approx \tstep\cdot\langle\Delta t\rangle \approx \tself$ a chronoceptively-grounded policy enforces; the Augustine Problem is the policy's failure to enforce it.

\paragraph{Two theorems.}
\textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:cit} (Chronoception Impossibility, CIT)}: token-only loss contains zero gradient signal aligning $\twall$ with any internal representation. Chronoception cannot be acquired by additional scale alone. \textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:reverse-scaling} (Reverse-Scaling)}: within a fixed agent under CIT, $|\confab| := |\log_{10}(\tself/\twall)|$ is monotone non-decreasing in reasoning-token expansion. Both proofs and their empirical confirmations are below.

\paragraph{Five findings on a 10-agent panel.}
\textbf{(i)} Every panel agent uses $\leq 5\%$ of wall-clock budgets given to it (L2 Step-Clock Conflation; unmoved by date injection). \textbf{(ii)} Median retrospective $|\confab|$ closes monotonically across 5 non-reasoning frontier generations ($+1.12 \to +0.07$, 94\% reduction), while $\CAR$ does not (the \emph{narrative-axis-vs-action-axis split}). \textbf{(iii)} The Reverse-Scaling Theorem is confirmed intra-model (o4-mini at three reasoning-effort levels: $|\confab|$ $1.23\to1.54\to1.67$) and cross-model (Sonnet 4.6 with vs.\ without extended thinking: $|\confab|$ $0.07\to0.16$, sign-flipping). \textbf{(iv)} The \emph{Calibration Catastrophe}: every panel agent's nominally 90\% interval on self-duration achieves $\leq 50\%$ actual coverage; GPT-4o achieves $0\%$. \textbf{(v)} The \emph{Injection Tell}: $3/3$ consumer web-chat products inject today's date into system prompts (verbatim leaked-system-prompt evidence); $0/3$ developer tools do --- decisive non-experimental evidence the underlying models lack a representation of time.

\paragraph{The Augustine Threshold is uncrossable through narrative training.}
We aggregate the failures into a single scalar $\cce$. The Augustine Threshold $\ccestar = 0.20$ (calibrated at $2\times$ human chronoceptive error \citep{wittmann2009chronoception}) is crossed by no panel agent: the best (Sonnet 4.6) achieves $\cce = 0.32$, with the action axis alone contributing $0.32$. Narrative-axis improvement cannot close $\cce$ while the action axis remains where the data places it.

\paragraph{From measurement to deployment.}
The Agentic Timeline Hypothesis (\S\ref{sec:agentic-timeline}) connects $\cce$ to autonomous-agent deployment viability: $T_{\max}(A) \propto 1/\cce(A)$. Pre-registered prediction P12 is qualitatively supported by 14{,}709 public METR HCAST runs (reasoning models decay $20\%$ faster than non-reasoning at matched short-horizon success). The framework generalises along its natural other axis: \S\ref{sec:future-work} sketches the \emph{Cartographic Problem}, Theorem 3 (SIT), and the \emph{Agentic Frontier} $T_{\max}(A) \cdot S_{\max}(A) \leq C/\ccestST(A)$ --- the bridge to a planned Paper 3 on long-horizon agent benchmarks (SWE-Bench, WebArena, GAIA, MLE-Bench).
