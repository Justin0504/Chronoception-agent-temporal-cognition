\section{The Agentic Timeline Hypothesis}
\label{sec:agentic-timeline}

The empirical sections establish that the Augustine Problem is real, measurable, and structurally bounded by L2. We close the framework's loop from measurement to deployment.

\begin{hypothesis}[Agentic Timeline]
\label{hyp:agentic-timeline}
For agent $A$ with chronoceptive calibration error $\cce(A)$ and median Clock-Adherence Ratio $\CAR(A)$, the maximum viable deployment horizon satisfies
$T_{\max}(A) \propto 1/\cce(A) \cdot f(\CAR(A))$,
with $f$ monotone-increasing in $\CAR$.
\end{hypothesis}

Combined with the empirical finding that L2 does not scale with capability (\S\ref{sec:l2}, the narrative-axis-vs-action-axis split), Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:agentic-timeline} implies $T_{\max}$ does not scale with capability either. Capability scaling closes the narrative axis (L3, \S\ref{sec:l3}) but leaves the action axis (L2) untouched; the Reverse-Scaling Theorem (\S\ref{ssec:reverse-scaling}) then says reasoning-token expansion \emph{degrades} the narrative axis. The autonomous-agent timeline is bounded by chronoception, not by intelligence or reasoning compute.

\textbf{Pre-registered P12.} On horizon-stratified benchmarks, the success-rate decay slope satisfies $\mathrm{slope}(\Delta\,\text{success rate} / \Delta\log T) \propto -(1 - \CAR(A))$.

\textbf{Supportive evidence on METR HCAST.} A definitive P12 test requires $\CAR$ on reasoning models, which we have deferred (T2.3 is prohibitively expensive at reasoning latencies). However the public HCAST corpus (14{,}709 runs, 20 frontier models) admits a qualitative test: \textbf{reasoning models should exhibit steeper success-rate decay than non-reasoning models at matched short-horizon success}, because the same L2 + Theorem 2 mechanisms apply at the class level (Figure~\ref{fig:p12-hcast}).

\textbf{Field implications.} (1) The narrative-axis ceiling is not the deployment ceiling: scaling along the narrative axis improves short-horizon experience but saturates against L2 at the few-hour horizon. (2) Provider injection (\S\ref{sec:injection-tell}) is a consumer-tier band-aid: it closes T1.1 at the chat tier but leaves L2 and L3 intact. (3) The first lab to install chronoception (via wall-clock-supported loss, learned-tool interfaces, or architectural primitives) shifts the deployment frontier --- the constructive program of \textsc{ChronoStack} (Paper 2).
