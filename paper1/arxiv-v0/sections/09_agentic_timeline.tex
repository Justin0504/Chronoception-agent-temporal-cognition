\section{The Agentic Timeline Hypothesis}
\label{sec:agentic-timeline}

The empirical results in \S\ref{sec:l2}--\S\ref{sec:injection-tell} establish that the Augustine Problem is real, measurable, and structurally bounded by L2. This section articulates the framework's bridge from measurement to deployment: \emph{why chronoception is the rate-limiting capability for the autonomous-agent trajectory the field is actively pursuing}.

\paragraph{Horizon-dependent chronoceptive requirements.}
As autonomous agent products move from minute-scale chat completion to hour-scale coding agents to day-scale autonomous research agents, each horizon $T$ imposes its own chronoceptive demands. Table~\ref{tab:horizon-requirements} summarises the requirement structure.

\paragraph{The Agentic Timeline Hypothesis.}
Let $\ccestar(T)$ denote the chronoceptive calibration error required for an autonomous agent to be viable at deployment horizon $T$. We hypothesise that $\ccestar(T)$ is monotone-decreasing in $T$. Equivalently, every agent has a \textbf{maximum viable horizon} $T_{\max}(\agent)$, beyond which its chronoceptive failures compound and task success decays to chance.

\begin{hypothesis}[Agentic Timeline Hypothesis]
\label{hyp:agentic-timeline}
For an agent $\agent$ with chronoceptive calibration error $\cce(\agent)$ and median Clock-Adherence Ratio $\CAR(\agent)$, the maximum viable deployment horizon $T_{\max}(\agent)$ satisfies
\[
T_{\max}(\agent) \;\propto\; \frac{1}{\cce(\agent)} \;\cdot\; f(\CAR(\agent)),
\]
where $f$ is monotonically increasing in $\CAR$.
\end{hypothesis}

The structural consequence of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:agentic-timeline} combined with our empirical Prediction P11 (\S\ref{sec:l2}: L2 does not scale with capability) is that \textbf{$T_{\max}$ does not scale with capability either}. Capability scaling closes the narrative axis (L3) but leaves the action axis (L2) untouched, so the action-axis component of $\cce$ remains the binding constraint at deployment-relevant horizons.

\paragraph{Connection to METR HCAST.}
\citet{kwa2025metrhorizons}'s HCAST benchmark measures agent success rate on tasks stratified by human time difficulty. The published capability-scaling curve exhibits two features that prior work has not jointly explained: (i) monotone improvement at short horizons; (ii) saturation at long horizons. The narrative-axis vs action-axis split (\S\ref{sec:l2}) supplies the mechanism: capability scaling closes L3 (driving short-horizon improvement) but leaves L2 bounded by the chronoception impossibility of \S\ref{sec:augustine-problem} (capping long-horizon ceiling).

\paragraph{Pre-registered Prediction P12.}
We formalise the connection as a falsifiable claim against horizon-stratified benchmarks.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{P12 (Agentic Timeline).} For an agent panel evaluated on horizon-stratified tasks (METR HCAST or analogues), the slope of success-rate decay with $\log T$ is proportional to $-(1 - \CAR(\agent))$ at fixed parameter count:
\[
\mathrm{slope}\!\left(\frac{\Delta\,\text{success rate}}{\Delta\log T}\right) \;\propto\; -(1 - \CAR(\agent)).
\]
Agents with $\CAR$ closer to $1$ lose less success rate per unit of horizon increase.
\end{quote}

P12 is the framework's deployment-relevant falsifiable claim. If P12 holds, chronoception (specifically the L2 component of $\cce$) is the explanatory variable for the slope of the autonomous-agent capability curve at long horizons. If P12 fails, the framework's deployment-relevance claim weakens.

\paragraph{Implications for the field.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{The narrative-axis ceiling is not the deployment ceiling.} The industry is scaling along the narrative axis (longer reasoning, better instruction-following, longer context). This strategy improves short-horizon experience but hits a ceiling on tasks beyond the few-hour horizon. Capability scaling alone will not save the autonomous-agent product line.
    \item \textbf{Provider injection is a consumer-tier band-aid.} The Injection Tell (\S\ref{sec:injection-tell}) closes T1.1 at the consumer product layer but leaves the deeper L2/L3 failure modes intact. Injection scales user trust at the chat tier; it does not scale autonomy at the agent tier.
    \item \textbf{The first lab to install chronoception shifts the deployment frontier.} CIT (\S\ref{sec:augustine-problem}) identifies three installation routes: loss terms with wall-clock support, inference-time tools with learned policy interfaces, and architectural primitives. We propose all three in a companion paper (\textsc{ChronoStack}); any lab that executes any of the three before competitors does so at the timing of a deployment-frontier shift.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What this section claims, and what it does not.}
Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:agentic-timeline} and P12 connect $\cce$ to deployment viability. They do not claim (a) that $T_{\max}$ has a closed-form analytic expression, (b) that no other capability matters, or (c) that \textsc{ChronoStack} is the unique route to chronoceptive installation. The claim is that \emph{chronoception is the binding constraint at deployment-relevant horizons}, falsifiable by failure of P12 on a sufficiently large agent panel.
