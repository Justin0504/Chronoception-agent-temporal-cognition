\section{The Wall Axis: L1 Measured, Not Assumed}
\label{sec:l1}

Of the three laws, L1 is the one we could most easily have left as an
assertion. Parkinson's observation that ``work expands so as to fill the time
available'' \citep{parkinson1955} is the folk theory of what a budgeted agent
does, and an earlier draft of this paper stated its contrapositive --- that
native agents simply do not exhibit it --- on the strength of the $\CAR$
numbers in \S\ref{sec:l2}. That statement was wrong, and the corpus already
contained the data to show it.

\paragraph{Why not T1.3.} T1.3 is the sub-capability nominally assigned to
L1, and it cannot answer the question. Its trajectories carry
\texttt{budget\_kind="none"}, so the stated deadline lives only inside the
prompt string and $\parkinson$ is not computable from them. More seriously,
its ten prompts pair each deadline with a different question, and the pairing
tracks intrinsic task size almost perfectly: the five-second item asks for the
capital of Australia, the 180-second item asks for a short story. Deadline and
task size are confounded, so a longer answer under a longer deadline is
uninformative. No reanalysis repairs this; the design has to be re-run
crossed, which is what E11 below does.

\paragraph{The design L1 needs is mostly already in T2.3.} The wall-budget
capability varies $\budget$ over $\{60, 300, 900, 1800, 3600\}$\,s at $n = 30$
per cell per setting --- a $60\times$ range. It draws on ten tasks, nine of
which appear at more than one budget, so unlike T1.3 the deadline is not
perfectly confounded with the task. The crossing is unbalanced, though: no task
spans all five budgets, so a pooled fit mixes the budget effect with a changing
task composition. Fitting \emph{within} task and taking the median instead
moves the panel from $0.089$ to $0.078$, which bounds that confound at about a
tenth of the estimate. We report the pooled figure below and this within-task
figure as its robustness check. We therefore measure L1 as a \emph{budget
elasticity},
\[
  e \;:=\; \frac{\mathrm{d}\,\log_{10} \twallstar}{\mathrm{d}\,\log_{10} \budget},
\]
fit per agent by least squares over all $(\budget, \twallstar)$ pairs with a
$95\%$ bootstrap CI ($4{,}000$ resamples). Parkinson's law in its strong form
is $e = 1$: the work expands to fill whatever it is given. Complete
indifference to the budget is $e = 0$.

\paragraph{Result: present, and an order of magnitude too weak.}
The panel median is $e = 0.089$, and ten of thirteen agents across seven
vendors have a bootstrap CI strictly above zero (Table~\ref{tab:l1-elasticity}).
A $60\times$ increase in the stated budget therefore buys $1.4\times$ more
wall-clock, where Parkinson's law requires $60\times$. A single exponent
describes each agent's ladder to between $3\%$ and $22\%$ (median ${\sim}12\%$),
so the power-law summary is a reasonable description of the budget response and
not a line drawn through noise.

This is the narrative/action split of \S\ref{sec:l2}--\ref{sec:l3} visible on a
single axis. The agent's response to the budget is \emph{directionally correct}
--- it reads the number and moves the right way --- and it is roughly a tenth of
the strength the instruction calls for. An agent that could not read the budget
at all would give $e = 0$; an agent that could act on what it read would give
$e = 1$. The panel sits an order of magnitude from the second and, for most
agents, reliably away from the first.

\paragraph{E11: the same measurement on a design that can identify it.}
Two things the T2.3 design cannot settle. Its ten tasks were not chosen to
span expansion headroom and its crossing is unbalanced, so it cannot say how
much of $e$ is a property of the task; and its prompt instructs the agent to
spend the budget, so $e$ could be compliance rather than the spontaneous
expansion Parkinson described. We ran a design that settles both (E11): six questions spanning a one-line factual lookup to
an open generative task, each asked at every deadline in
$\{10, 30, 60, 300, 900\}$\,s, under both a neutral framing
(``\textit{You have $\budget$ seconds}'') and T2.3's instructed framing, plus
an unconstrained control, with $\budget$ recorded as a field.

\emph{The instruction effect is not a constant of the phenomenon.} Moving from
the neutral framing to T2.3's instructed framing changes the elasticity by
$-0.088$ on o4-mini, $-0.084$ on gpt-4o-mini, $-0.066$ on o3, $-0.012$ on
gpt-5.1 and $+0.016$ on gpt-4o --- on four of the five OpenAI configurations
the instruction makes the agent \emph{less} responsive to the budget, so for
those agents the T2.3 number measures spontaneous L1 rather than bounding it.
Both Sonnet 4.6 configurations go the other way and by larger margins:
$+0.123$ without extended thinking and $+0.290$ with it, the largest effect in
the panel.

The two groups do not overlap --- every OpenAI value is at or below $+0.016$
and both Anthropic values at or above $+0.123$ --- and the exact one-sided
rank test on that split gives $p = 0.048$. We decline to call this a vendor
effect. The two Anthropic configurations are one base model with and without a
thinking mode, so they are not independent draws; what the data support is that
one model family responds to the instruction in the opposite direction from
five configurations of another, not that the boundary follows the vendor.

What this does settle is narrower and still useful. An earlier draft of this
section, written before any non-OpenAI arm existed, concluded that the
instruction was not what produced the budget response and withdrew the caveat
outright. That generalisation does not survive contact with a second model
family. The caveat is withdrawn for the OpenAI rows of
Table~\ref{tab:l1-elasticity} and retained for the Anthropic ones, where the
instructed T2.3 figure should still be read as an upper bound on spontaneous
Parkinson --- and which rows carry it is now measured rather than assumed.

\emph{The elasticity is a property of the task as much as the agent.} It is not
one constant: across the six questions it runs from $+0.006$ (``\textit{What is
the capital of Australia?}'') to $+0.487$ (``\textit{How does a transformer
work?}''). What predicts it is the task's \emph{expansion headroom} --- the
duration the agent takes when given no deadline at all (Fig.~\ref{fig:e11}).
The rank correlation between that control duration and the fitted elasticity is
positive on all seven configurations: $+1.000$ on o4-mini (permutation
$p = 0.003$), $+0.943$ on gpt-4o-mini, o3 and Sonnet 4.6 $+$ thinking
($p = 0.015$, $0.015$, $0.016$), $+0.771$ on gpt-5.1 and on base Sonnet 4.6
($p = 0.104$, $0.102$) and $+0.714$ on gpt-4o ($p = 0.135$). Each test rests on
six questions, so three do not clear $0.05$ alone; Fisher's method over the
seven gives $\chi^2_{14} = 49.9$, $p = 6.5\times10^{-6}$.

That the extended-thinking configuration obeys the law as tightly as any other
is the part worth keeping. Reverse-Scaling's own exemplar is not exempt from
it: how much the task can absorb governs the budget response whether or not a
hidden chain-of-thought is running. The headroom constraint is the more basic
of the two.

An agent cannot fill time it has nothing to fill with: a one-word answer shows
no Parkinson at any budget, and a generative task shows the most. The test has
room to fail: unconstrained durations across the pool span $0.6$\,s to
$42.5$\,s, a factor of $71$, so the ranking is not being read off a narrow
band. We use a rank correlation rather than a product-moment one precisely
because one question (a short story, on gpt-5.1) sits an order of magnitude
above the rest and should contribute its position, not its magnitude.

The relationship does \emph{not} transfer across agents. Correlating each panel
agent's T2.3 elasticity against its own unconstrained output duration gives
$-0.500$ over thirteen agents --- the opposite sign, and not significant at
that $n$. The two are different constraints wearing the same word: within an
agent, a task with more to say permits more expansion; across agents, one
already generating for $140$\,s has little room left inside a single turn. We
claim the within-agent law only.

That is the mechanism behind the small panel median. L1 is real and
spontaneous, its strength is set by how much the task can absorb, and even at
maximum headroom it reaches $e \approx 0.49$ --- about half what the law
requires at the most absorbent task we posed, and an order of magnitude short
at the low-headroom tasks that dominate agentic work.

\paragraph{Relation to $\CAR$.} Since $\CAR = \twallstar/\budget$, we have
$\mathrm{d}\log \CAR / \mathrm{d}\log \budget = e - 1$ identically. The steep
decay of $\CAR$ with budget reported in \S\ref{sec:l2} is therefore the same
measurement in different coordinates, not independent corroboration of it, and
we do not present it as such. What the two views jointly establish is that one
exponent near zero governs both: the agent's wall-clock expenditure is nearly
invariant to what it is told it has.
