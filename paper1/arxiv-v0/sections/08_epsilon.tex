\section{Chronoceptive Calibration Error $\cce$}
\label{sec:epsilon}

We aggregate the three axes into a single scalar.

\begin{definition}[Chronoceptive Calibration Error]
\label{def:cce}
$\cce(A) := \tfrac{1}{3}(\mathrm{score}_{T_1} + \mathrm{score}_{T_2} + \mathrm{score}_{T_3})$, where each axis-score $\mathrm{score}_{T_k}$ is the mean of the available sub-capability failure scores on that axis, normalised to $[0, 1]$ (Appendix~\ref{app:full-panel-tables}). $\cce = 0$ corresponds to a grounded agent; $\ccestar = 0.20$ is the Augustine threshold, calibrated against human introspective performance \citep{wittmann2009chronoception} at twice the human error.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Aggregation disclosure.}
The full benchmark defines nine sub-capabilities (T1.1--T3.3, \S\ref{sec:chronobench}). On the panel-wide pilot, axis-scores reduce to the three primary sub-capabilities for which we have full $n{=}30$ panel coverage --- T1.1 (date recall), T2.3 (clock adherence), T3.1 (retrospective duration) --- because T1.2, T1.3, T2.1, T2.2, T3.2, T3.3 are only filled for the five-generation non-reasoning slice (see Table~\ref{tab:full-panel}, App.~B). Where a sub-capability is missing for a given agent (e.g., o3 / o4-mini T1.1 on Setting~A), that sub-capability is dropped from the axis mean rather than imputed; consequently the $\cce$ point estimates in Figure~\ref{fig:epsilon-panel} are pilot-axis estimates, not full nine-cap averages. The pilot-axis estimate is a lower bound on the full-nine-cap $\cce$ whenever the dropped cells exhibit comparable or worse failure (which all per-cell evidence so far supports). Per-cell $n$ and bootstrap CIs in App.~B make this aggregation auditable.

\paragraph{Two findings.}
\textbf{(1) The action axis is the binding constraint.} Among the four agents that sit closer to the threshold than $0.20$ above it (Sonnet 4.6, gpt-5.1, Haiku 4.5, o3), $\mathrm{score}_{T_2}$ dominates the aggregate: it contributes at least $0.317$ on every agent. Even projecting $\mathrm{score}_{T_3}$ to zero --- which would require crossing both Theorem~\ref{thm:cit} and Theorem~\ref{thm:reverse-scaling} --- no agent crosses $\ccestar$. The Augustine threshold is unreachable through narrative training.

\textbf{(2) Reverse-Scaling operates on the aggregate.} The o4-mini effort sweep takes $\cce$ from $0.470$ at low effort to $0.532$ at medium to $0.611$ at high. The Sonnet 4.6 thinking comparison takes $\cce$ from $0.276$ to $0.301$. More reasoning compute moves the agent further from the threshold, not closer. The two findings combine into a single structural claim: there is no axis along which token-only training can close the gap.
