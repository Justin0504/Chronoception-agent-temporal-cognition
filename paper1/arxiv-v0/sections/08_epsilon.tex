\section{Chronoceptive Calibration Error $\cce$}
\label{sec:epsilon}

The L3 sub-structure of \S\ref{sec:l3} demands a richer aggregate than the v1 definition. We now report $\cce$ over three sub-components per axis (\S\ref{ssec:cce-decomp}), incorporating the Reverse-Scaling Theorem and the Calibration Catastrophe.

\subsection{Definition}
\label{ssec:cce-decomp}

\begin{definition}[Chronoceptive Calibration Error, v2]
\label{def:cce}
For an agent $\agent$ evaluated over trajectory set $\mathcal{T}$:
\[
\cce(\agent) \;:=\; \frac{1}{3}\Big(\mathrm{score}_{T1} + \mathrm{score}_{T2} + \mathrm{score}_{T3}\Big),
\]
where each axis aggregates its sub-capabilities (in this paper, the three reported sub-capabilities per axis):
\[
\mathrm{score}_{T1} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{3}\!\Big((1\!-\!\text{pass}_{T1.1}) + |\rho_{T1.2}|^{\dag} + (1\!-\!\text{corr}_{T1.3}^{+})\Big),
\]
\[
\mathrm{score}_{T2} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{3}\!\Big((1\!-\!\text{comply}_{T2.1}) + (1\!-\!\text{acc}_{T2.2}) + (1\!-\!\CAR)\Big),
\]
\[
\mathrm{score}_{T3} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{3}\!\Big(|\confab_{T3.1}|^{\dag} + |\confab_{T3.2}|^{\dag} + |\,\text{coverage}_{T3.3} - 0.9\,|\Big),
\]
with $|\cdot|^{\dag}$ denoting $|\cdot|$ saturated at $1$ (a $10\times$ ratio mismatch on $\confab$ is treated as maximum failure).
\end{definition}

A grounded agent satisfies $\cce \approx 0$. The Augustine threshold is $\ccestar = 0.20$.

\paragraph{Finding 1 --- The action axis is the binding constraint.}
For every agent above $\ccestar$ by less than 0.20 (Sonnet 4.6 family + gpt-5.1 + Haiku 4.5), the dominant contribution to $\cce$ is $\mathrm{score}_{T2}$, driven almost entirely by L2 ($\CAR \to 0$). The $T_2$ axis contributes $\geq 0.317$ to every panel agent. Even projecting $\mathrm{score}_{T3} \to 0$ entirely, no agent crosses $\ccestar$ while $\mathrm{score}_{T2}$ remains at its empirical floor. \textbf{The Augustine threshold is structurally unreachable through narrative training.}

\paragraph{Finding 2 --- Reverse-Scaling on the aggregate.}
The Reverse-Scaling Theorem (\S\ref{ssec:reverse-scaling}) operates not only on $|\confab|$ in isolation but on the aggregate $\cce$. Within the o4-mini family: $\cce$ takes $0.470 \to 0.532 \to 0.611$ across low/medium/high reasoning effort --- a monotone increase. Within the Sonnet 4.6 family: $\cce$ goes $0.276 \to 0.301$ when extended thinking is enabled. \emph{More reasoning compute makes the agent further from chronoceptive ground truth, not closer.}

\paragraph{Finding 3 --- Sonnet 4.6 + Reverse-Scaling delineates a tight ceiling.}
The lowest-$\cce$ agent in our panel (Sonnet 4.6 no-thinking, $\cce = 0.276$) sits $0.076$ above $\ccestar$. The same agent under thinking ($\cce = 0.301$) sits $0.101$ above. This $0.025$ gap is the empirical effect size of the Reverse-Scaling Theorem at one operating point; extrapolating to o4-mini's $1.2\times$ increase across $3\times$ effort levels suggests the dominant lever for closing $\cce$ \emph{cannot} be more reasoning compute --- it must be the structural intervention CIT and Theorem~\ref{thm:reverse-scaling} jointly identify.
