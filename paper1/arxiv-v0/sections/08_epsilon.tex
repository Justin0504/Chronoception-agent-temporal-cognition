\section{The Chronoceptive Calibration Error $\cce$}
\label{sec:epsilon}

We aggregate the three axes into a single scalar for cross-agent comparison and the deployment bound of \S\ref{sec:agentic-timeline}.

\begin{definition}[Chronoceptive Calibration Error]
\label{def:cce}
$\cce(A) := \tfrac{1}{3}(\mathrm{score}_{T_1} + \mathrm{score}_{T_2} + \mathrm{score}_{T_3})$, where each axis-score is the mean of available sub-capability failure scores normalised to $[0, 1]$ (App.~\ref{app:full-panel-tables}). $\cce = 0$ is grounded; $\ccestar = 0.20$ is the Augustine threshold. Missing cells are dropped, not imputed; the pilot-axis estimate is a lower bound on the full nine-cap $\cce$ where dropped cells exhibit comparable-or-worse failure.
\end{definition}

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/epsilon_panel.pdf}
\caption{Panel $\cce$ ranking. No agent crosses $\ccestar$. The best agent (Sonnet 4.6 $+$ thinking, $\cce = 0.276$) sits $1.4\times$ above; its T2 axis alone contributes $\geq 0.30$, irreducible by any narrative-axis intervention. Full sub-capability decomposition in App.~\ref{app:l3-detail}.}
\label{fig:epsilon-panel}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Two findings.} \textbf{(1) The action axis is binding.} Among the four agents closer to $\ccestar$ than $0.20$ above it (Sonnet~4.6 $\pm$ thinking, gpt-5.1, Haiku 4.5), $\mathrm{score}_{T_2}$ contributes $\geq 0.30$ on every agent. Projecting $\mathrm{score}_{T_3} \to 0$ --- which would require crossing both Theorem~\ref{thm:cit} and Theorem~\ref{thm:reverse-scaling} --- still leaves no agent below $\ccestar$. \textbf{(2) Reverse-Scaling operates on the aggregate.} The o4-mini effort sweep takes $\cce$ from $0.470$ (low) to $0.611$ (high). More reasoning compute moves the agent further from the threshold. There is no axis along which token-only training can close the gap.

This is the paper's central empirical claim. The next section turns it into a deployment bound.
