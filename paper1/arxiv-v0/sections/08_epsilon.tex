\section{Chronoceptive Calibration Error $\cce$}
\label{sec:epsilon}

We aggregate the three-axis failures into a single scalar.

\begin{definition}[Chronoceptive Calibration Error]
\label{def:cce}
$\cce(A) := \frac{1}{3}(\mathrm{score}_{T_1} + \mathrm{score}_{T_2} + \mathrm{score}_{T_3})$, where each axis is the mean of its sub-capability failure scores normalised to $[0,1]$ (see Appendix~\ref{app:full-panel-tables}). $\cce = 0$ is grounded; $\ccestar = 0.20$ is the Augustine threshold (calibrated at $2\times$ human introspective error \citep{wittmann2009chronoception}).
\end{definition}

\label{fig:epsilon-panel}

\textbf{Two findings.} \textbf{(1)} The action axis is the binding constraint. For every agent above $\ccestar$ by less than $0.20$ (Sonnet 4.6, gpt-5.1, Haiku 4.5), $\mathrm{score}_{T_2}$ dominates: T2 alone contributes $\geq 0.317$. Even projecting $\mathrm{score}_{T_3} \to 0$, no agent crosses $\ccestar$. \textbf{The Augustine Threshold is structurally unreachable through narrative training.} \textbf{(2)} Reverse-Scaling operates on aggregate $\cce$: o4-mini effort-sweep $0.470 \to 0.532 \to 0.611$; Sonnet 4.6 baseline $\to$ thinking $0.276 \to 0.301$. More reasoning compute makes the agent strictly further from grounded.
