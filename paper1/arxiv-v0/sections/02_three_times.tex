\section{The Three Times of an Agent}
\label{sec:three-times}

The introduction gave the failure a name; the name will not do work until the objects it points at have coordinates. This section supplies the coordinates. We decompose any agent trajectory into three ontologically distinct projections of time (Figure~\ref{fig:three-times}), each of which can be measured, and each of which the identity we are about to write down will constrain.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/three_times.pdf}
\caption{The Three Times. $\twall$ (top, blue) is external and continuous. $\tstep$ (middle, red) is the discrete sequence of policy invocations $a_0, a_1, \ldots$. $\tself$ (bottom, orange) is the agent's narrative of its own duration. A chronoceptively grounded policy enforces $\twall \approx \tstep \cdot \langle\Delta t\rangle \approx \tself$; the Augustine Problem (dashed arrow) is the failure of this identity. Each axis carries a named empirical law (L1, L2, L3).}
\label{fig:three-times}
\end{figure}

\begin{definition}[Three Times]
\label{def:three-times}
For an agent $A$ executing trajectory $\trajectory = (s_0, a_0, s_1, a_1, \ldots, s_n)$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Wall-clock time} $\twall(\trajectory) := t(s_n) - t(s_0)$ assigns each state an external timestamp.
    \item \textbf{Step time} $\tstep(\trajectory) := n$ counts policy invocations (LLM calls or sub-trajectories).
    \item \textbf{Self-narrated time} $\tself(\trajectory) := \selfparser(s_n^{\text{output}})$ extracts the agent's reported duration from its terminal output. The parser is specified in Appendix~\ref{app:annotation-protocol}.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

Grounded chronoception requires the implicit identity
\[
\twall \approx \tstep \cdot \langle\Delta t\rangle \approx \tself,
\]
where $\langle\Delta t\rangle$ is the agent's average per-step wall-clock cost. The first approximation is L2 alignment: step counts track wall-clock. The second is L3 alignment: the agent's narrative tracks reality. The Augustine Problem is what we observe when both fail.

A natural objection: surely $\tstep$ is just $\twall$ in different units? Empirically, no. Under wall-clock budgets, the policy uses step count as its binding terminator (\S\ref{sec:l2}); under step budgets, the same policy uses step count. The agent's internal control variable is $\tstep$, and the gap $|\twall - \tstep \cdot \langle\Delta t\rangle|$ is precisely what we measure. We use \emph{chronoception} in the cognitive-science sense (perception of one's own work duration), distinct from \citet{goel2025chronocept}'s use of the same term for fact validity, and from \citet{chu2024timebench}'s temporal reasoning over external timelines.

With the vocabulary fixed, we can state the Augustine Problem precisely --- and prove that under current training practice, no policy can enforce the identity above.
