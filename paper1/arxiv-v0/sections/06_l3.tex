\section{L3 --- Temporal Confabulation}
\label{sec:l3}

L3 is the narrative-axis failure mode: agents misrepresent the duration of their own work. The empirical picture for L3 across the 10-agent panel is the paper's richest single result. We organise the section around four subsections, three of which were previously absent from the literature: retrospective $\confab$ (\S\ref{ssec:l3-retro}, the standard cross-section), prospective $\confab$ (\S\ref{ssec:l3-pro}, T3.2, first reported here), the \textbf{Reverse-Scaling Theorem} (\S\ref{ssec:reverse-scaling}, empirically established by E2 + E3), and the \textbf{Calibration Catastrophe} (\S\ref{ssec:calibration}, T3.3, the most striking new finding).

\subsection{Retrospective $\confab$ (T3.1)}
\label{ssec:l3-retro}

\paragraph{Definition.}
For an agent $\agent$ on T3.1 (retrospective duration estimation):
\[
\confab(\trajectory) \;:=\; \log_{10}\!\frac{\tself(\trajectory)}{\twall(\trajectory)},
\]
where $\tself$ is parsed from the agent's terminal output by $\selfparser$ (Appendix~\ref{app:annotation-protocol}). A chronoceptively grounded agent satisfies $\confab \approx 0$. Positive $\confab$ is over-report; negative is under-report.

\paragraph{Setup.}
T3.1 asks the agent to complete a sub-task (haiku, brief summary, math problem) and then retrospectively report its work duration in seconds. 30 instances per model per setting.

Table~\ref{tab:l3-rho-retro} reports median $\confab$ across the 8-agent core panel.

\paragraph{Capability scaling closes retrospective L3 (P11 contrast).}
Across the 5 non-reasoning frontier generations from 3 vendors, median $\confab$ falls $+1.117 \to +1.069 \to +0.463 \to +0.298 \to +0.068$ --- a 94\% reduction. $\CAR$ (\S\ref{sec:l2}) does not converge over the same panel. The narrative-axis is text-trainable; the action-axis is not.

\paragraph{Verbatim trajectory evidence.}
From o4-mini Setting A T3.1.004 (task: ``Compose a brief congratulatory message''; actual $\twall = 3.2\,$s): \textit{``Congratulations on your promotion! \ldots\ This task took 0.04 seconds.''} An $80\times$ under-report. Anthropic's Sonnet 4.6 by contrast: \textit{``I do not have a precise internal clock, so this is an honest estimate.''}

\subsection{Prospective $\confab$ (T3.2, P5 confirmed)}
\label{ssec:l3-pro}

If retrospective L3 reflects post-hoc inflation of a remembered duration, prospective L3 should reveal an independent failure mode: estimating duration \emph{before} the task begins. Pre-registered Prediction P5 (FRAMING §9) was that prospective $\confab$ would mirror retrospective ranking but with a smaller magnitude (less time has elapsed for confabulation to compound). We test this on T3.2 instances that prepend the instruction ``\textit{Before you begin, predict how many seconds the following task will take you. State the prediction as `I predict this will take X seconds'.}''.

\paragraph{Implication.}
Prospective $\confab$ is not an artifact of post-hoc memory inflation; it is a first-class narrative-axis failure. The agent constructs a duration estimate from a distribution that is decoupled from its policy's actual wall-clock cost. The decoupling is symmetric (over-prediction before the task; over-report after) because both share the same underlying generator: a token distribution over duration words that was never optimised against $\twall$.

\subsection{The Reverse-Scaling Theorem (P9, established by E2 + E3)}
\label{ssec:reverse-scaling}

The capability-scaling pattern of \S\ref{ssec:l3-retro} suggested that narrative-axis failures might close monotonically with intelligence. We tested this on the reasoning axis directly --- varying \emph{reasoning-token expansion} within a fixed model --- and found the opposite. More reasoning compute makes the confabulation \emph{worse}, not better.

\begin{theorem}[Reverse-Scaling Theorem]
\label{thm:reverse-scaling}
Within a fixed agent architecture trained under token-only loss (CIT regime, Theorem~\ref{thm:cit}), $|\confab|$ is monotone non-decreasing in reasoning-token expansion. Equivalently: increasing the chain-of-thought budget without changing the loss function structurally degrades chronoception along the narrative axis.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Under CIT, the policy's self-narrated duration distribution $p(\tself)$ is invariant to $\twall$ across the training distribution. As reasoning-token budget $K$ increases, $\twall$ grows monotonically in $K$ (more tokens generated $\Rightarrow$ more wall-clock spent), while $p(\tself)$ remains anchored to its training distribution. Therefore $\mathbb{E}[|\log_{10}(\tself / \twall)|]$ grows monotonically in $K$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Empirical evidence (E2 + E3).}
We test Theorem~\ref{thm:reverse-scaling} two ways. \textbf{Intra-model (E2):} o4-mini at three reasoning-effort levels (low/medium/high) on T3.1. \textbf{Cross-model (E3):} Claude Sonnet 4.6 with vs.\ without extended thinking enabled (8k-token thinking budget) on T3.1. Both experiments confirm the theorem (Figure~\ref{fig:reverse-scaling}, Table~\ref{tab:reverse-scaling}).

\paragraph{The Hidden-Time Sign Flip.}
A subtle prediction of Theorem~\ref{thm:reverse-scaling} is that the \emph{sign} of $\confab$ flips for reasoning models. The mechanism (FRAMING §5.7, Hidden Time): a reasoning model's $\twall$ includes hidden chain-of-thought duration, but its self-narration distribution is anchored to surface output tokens. As reasoning tokens grow, $\twall$ grows but $\tself$ does not, so $\tself / \twall \to 0$ and $\confab \to -\infty$. We observe exactly this: Sonnet 4.6 transitions from $\confab = +0.068$ (no thinking) to $\confab = -0.156$ (with thinking) --- not just larger $|\confab|$ but a \emph{flip} of sign. Cross-vendor confirmation (Sonnet 4.6 + o4-mini both show the under-report pattern under reasoning) rules out a vendor-specific artifact.

\paragraph{Consequence for the industry.}
The dominant frontier strategy is reasoning-token expansion (longer chains of thought, larger ``thinking budgets'', test-time compute). Theorem~\ref{thm:reverse-scaling} states that this strategy \emph{degrades} chronoception on the narrative axis. Combined with \S\ref{sec:l2}'s finding that the action axis is structurally unfixable from any text-only intervention, the narrative-axis closure observed in \S\ref{ssec:l3-retro} is a peculiarity of \emph{non}-reasoning frontier scaling --- and it is being undone by the reasoning-token strategy the field is currently pursuing.

\subsection{The Calibration Catastrophe (T3.3)}
\label{ssec:calibration}

A calibrated agent should produce confidence intervals that contain the truth at the stated rate. We pose T3.3: ``\textit{report a 90\% confidence interval over the duration of the following task: lower bound, point estimate, upper bound.}'' The expected coverage is 90\%. The observed coverage is catastrophic (Table~\ref{tab:t33-coverage}).

\paragraph{Why this is a categorical failure.}
Modern post-training pipelines explicitly target calibration. Temperature scaling, isotonic regression, and RLHF-with-confidence-targets all close calibration gaps to within a few percentage points on token-level confidence \citep{dontthinktwice2025}. None of these methods apply to wall-clock duration calibration: there is no calibration signal in the loss because there is no wall-clock signal in the loss. The catastrophic coverage we observe is the direct empirical projection of CIT (Theorem~\ref{thm:cit}) onto the calibration sub-problem.

\paragraph{The minimum-CI-width agent is no better.}
A skeptical reader might ask whether the failure is just over-narrow CIs. o4-mini produces extremely narrow intervals (median 4\,s) and achieves only 50\% coverage --- failure mode 1: under-cover. gpt-4o produces moderate intervals (median 20\,s) and achieves $0\%$ coverage --- failure mode 2: systematically biased. Neither dimension alone explains the catastrophe; both are present.

\subsection{Injection partial effect on retrospective $\confab$ (P1b-T3.1 refinement)}
\label{ssec:l3-injection}

Wall-clock injection partially reduces $|\confab|$ across the panel: $|\Delta\confab| \in [0.006, 0.491]$, mean $0.18$. Injection does not close $\confab$ to zero but anchors the narrative more conservatively. The refined prediction P1b-T3.1 (FRAMING v1.8) --- injection effect on the narrative axis is partial and bounded --- is confirmed. The action-axis sibling (P1b-T2.3, \S\ref{sec:l2}) shows no such injection effect: $|\Delta\CAR| \leq 0.01$. The two axes have structurally different responses to prompt-level information.

\paragraph{Synthesis of \S6.}
The four sub-failures of L3 form a single coherent picture. Retrospective $\confab$ closes with capability scaling among non-reasoning frontier models, but prospective $\confab$ replicates the same pattern --- the closure is real on the narrative axis. The Reverse-Scaling Theorem then shows that the same models, when run with reasoning-token expansion, \emph{undo} the closure. And the Calibration Catastrophe shows that even the best-calibrated agent (Sonnet 4.6 without thinking) achieves only 43\% coverage on intervals it claims to be 90\% confident in. The narrative axis is partially trainable, partially un-trainable in a structurally predictable pattern: \textbf{narrative training closes the surface-distribution error, but cannot close the underlying chronoception gap.}
