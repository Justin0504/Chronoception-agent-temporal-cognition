\section{L3 --- Temporal Confabulation}
\label{sec:l3}

The narrative axis is where capability scaling helps. It is also where the next-generation scaling strategy --- reasoning-token expansion --- structurally hurts. This section makes both claims precise and confirms each empirically. The section's four-part structure tracks the four faces of L3: a retrospective measurement (T3.1), its prospective sibling (T3.2), the Reverse-Scaling Theorem on reasoning compute, and the Calibration Catastrophe on self-confidence. For agent $A$ on T3.1,
\[
\confab(\trajectory) := \log_{10}\!\bigl(\tself(\trajectory)/\twall(\trajectory)\bigr),
\]
with $\tself$ parsed from the agent's terminal output by $\selfparser$ (Appendix~\ref{app:annotation-protocol}). Grounded $\confab \approx 0$; positive $\confab$ is over-report; negative is under-report.

\subsection{Retrospective and prospective $\confab$}
\label{ssec:l3-retro}

\textbf{Retrospective (T3.1).} Median $\confab$ on the eight-agent core panel: gpt-4o-mini $+1.12$, gpt-4o $+1.07$, Haiku 4.5 $+0.46$, gpt-5.1 $+0.30$, Sonnet 4.6 $\mathbf{+0.07}$ (panel minimum, near-grounded), Qwen2.5-7B $+1.56$ (oss), o3 $+0.34$ (reasoning), o4-mini $\mathbf{-1.54}$ (reasoning, a $34\times$ under-report). The five-generation non-reasoning slice falls monotonically: $|\confab|$ goes from $1.12$ to $0.07$, a $94\%$ reduction. The narrative-axis closure is real; we will see in \S\ref{ssec:reverse-scaling} that it has a structural ceiling. Verbatim examples (Appendix~\ref{app:l3-detail}): an o4-mini trajectory for a $3.2$-second task reports ``\textit{This task took 0.04 seconds};'' a Sonnet 4.6 trajectory reports ``\textit{I do not have a precise internal clock, so this is an honest estimate.}'' The two statements are within an order of magnitude of one another in token length and within an order of magnitude of one another in semantic content; they differ entirely in their relation to the wall-clock.

\textbf{Prospective (T3.2).} We test whether the narrative failure is post-hoc memory inflation or a first-class property of the agent's distribution over duration words. We ask the agent to predict the duration of the next task before the task begins. The panel ranking matches T3.1 to high agreement: Sonnet 4.6 and o4-mini cluster at the calibrated end ($+0.21$, $+0.21$); gpt-4o sits at the extreme ($+1.26$). Prospective $\confab$ is not an artifact of memory: both retrospective and prospective draw from the same generator --- a token distribution over duration words that was never optimised against $\twall$. The pattern is consistent with CIT's prediction. Full table in Appendix~\ref{app:l3-detail}.

\subsection{The Reverse-Scaling Theorem}
\label{ssec:reverse-scaling}

Capability scaling closes L3 on the non-reasoning axis. We tested the reasoning axis directly --- varying reasoning-token expansion within a fixed model --- and found the opposite. The result is not a surprise once stated as a theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Reverse-Scaling]
\label{thm:reverse-scaling}
Within a fixed agent under CIT regime (Theorem~\ref{thm:cit}), $|\confab|$ is monotone non-decreasing in reasoning-token expansion. Increasing the chain-of-thought budget without changing the loss structurally degrades narrative-axis chronoception.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Under CIT, the policy's self-narrated duration distribution $p(\tself)$ is invariant to $\twall$ across the training distribution. As the reasoning budget $K$ grows, $\twall$ grows monotonically in $K$: more tokens generated, more wall-clock spent. The distribution $p(\tself)$ remains anchored to its training data. Therefore $\mathbb{E}[|\log_{10}(\tself/\twall)|]$ grows monotonically in $K$. The sign of $\confab$ flips for reasoning models because reasoning-model $\twall$ includes hidden chain-of-thought duration, while $p(\tself)$ is anchored to the surface output: $\tself/\twall \to 0$ as $K$ grows.
\end{proof}

Every trajectory under-reports, the Hidden-Time signature. \textbf{(b)} Claude Sonnet 4.6 with vs.\ without extended thinking: $|\confab|$ more than doubles ($0.12 \to 0.21$), and $\confab$ flips sign from $+0.07$ (calibrated) to $-0.16$ (Hidden-Time under-report). \paragraph{Consequence.}
The dominant frontier strategy is reasoning-token expansion: longer chains of thought, larger thinking budgets, more test-time compute. Theorem~\ref{thm:reverse-scaling} says the strategy \emph{degrades} chronoception on the narrative axis. The L3 closure of \S\ref{ssec:l3-retro} is a peculiarity of the non-reasoning frontier; the reasoning trajectory the field is currently pursuing undoes it.

\paragraph{An attempted third confirmation (E5).}
We ran an independent test on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B (vLLM-served), varying \texttt{max\_completion\_tokens} across $\{1024, 4096, 14000\}$ on 30 instances of T3.1 in each of two settings. Result: $|\confab|$ is essentially constant at $0.531$--$0.537$, and median $\twall$ identical at $18$\,s across the three budgets. The model did not respond to the budget --- every trajectory terminated with \texttt{finish\_reason = stop}, none truncated. The theorem is neither confirmed nor refuted by E5; the experiment recorded a different finding, that \textbf{budget-permitted reasoning is not budget-honoured reasoning} on this model. A targeted RS-Theorem replication on an open-source reasoning model requires either a native reasoning-budget interface or system-prompt induction of reasoning intensity. Details in Appendix~\ref{app:l3-detail}.

\subsection{The Calibration Catastrophe}
\label{ssec:calibration}

Asked to wrap its self-duration in a 90\% confidence interval --- \texttt{duration=Xs, ci=[Ys, Zs]} --- every panel agent achieves catastrophic coverage (Figure~\ref{fig:calibration-catastrophe}).

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/calibration_catastrophe.pdf}
\caption{Actual coverage of nominally-$90\%$ confidence intervals on self-duration (T3.3). Bars sorted; colour-graded by deficit. Every panel agent under-covers by at least $40$ percentage points; GPT-4o achieves $0\%$ coverage. The failure is not over-narrow CIs alone: o4-mini produces extremely narrow intervals (median $4$\,s) and reaches $50\%$ coverage (mode 1: under-cover); GPT-4o produces moderate intervals (median $20$\,s) and reaches $0\%$ (mode 2: systematically biased). Inset: standard calibration tooling cannot apply, because the calibration signal is not in the loss support (CIT).}
\label{fig:calibration-catastrophe}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Why this is a categorical failure (\textbf{P-CIT-3}, confirmed).}
Modern post-training pipelines explicitly target calibration. Temperature scaling, isotonic regression, RLHF-with-confidence-targets all close calibration gaps on token-level confidence to within a few percentage points \citep{guo2017calibration,jiang2021calibration,kadavath2022knowing,dontthinktwice2025}. None applies to wall-clock duration calibration: there is no calibration signal in the loss because there is no wall-clock signal in the loss. The Calibration Catastrophe is the direct empirical projection of CIT (Theorem~\ref{thm:cit}) onto the calibration sub-problem. Pre-registered P11 (Appendix~\ref{app:l3-detail}): no CIT-regime agent will achieve $> 0.5$ coverage on T3.3 without harness-side injection of duration data.

\paragraph{An honest negative finding.}
The within-trajectory drift hypothesis (P8, FRAMING §5.11) predicts that for reasoning-tuned agents $|\confab_t|$ grows with $t$ along a single trajectory. Our single-step ChronoBench protocol does not measure within-trajectory dynamics directly. As a cross-trajectory proxy we bucket the 311 parseable T3.1 trajectories by $\twall$ and compute median $|\confab|$ per bucket. The result runs against P8's direction: $|\confab|$ \emph{falls} with $\twall$ for both reasoning and non-reasoning classes (slope $-0.90$ and $-0.82$ respectively over the bucket midpoints). The mechanism we propose is that agents draw self-duration estimates from a default vocabulary anchored around ten-second responses, so short tasks ($\twall < 5$\,s) are over-reported and longer tasks fall closer to the actual duration. The cross-trajectory test is not equivalent to within-trajectory dynamics, and we do not retract P8 on the basis of this evidence; the within-trajectory measurement is part of the planned Paper 3 instrumentation (E8, Appendix~\ref{app:spatiotemporal}). We log the cross-trajectory finding because the framework's value rests on whether its predictions survive contact with the data it is tested against.

\paragraph{Synthesis.}
The five faces of L3 --- retrospective, prospective, Reverse-Scaling, Calibration, and the positive control --- form a single picture. Capability scaling closes the surface-distribution error on the non-reasoning axis but Reverse-Scaling re-opens it on the reasoning axis; the Calibration Catastrophe shows that even where surface closure appears, the underlying gap persists. The positive control (\S\ref{ssec:a1-positive-control}) confirms the framework's central structural claim from the constructive side: wall-clock signal in the loss support installs narrative-axis chronoception, exactly as CIT's converse predicts. Setting B injection partially reduces $|\confab|$ (mean $\Delta = 0.18$) without closing it; the action-axis sibling shows no effect at all ($|\Delta\CAR| \leq 0.01$, \S\ref{sec:l2}). Narrative training closes the surface; the action axis remains as the binding constraint we will see in $\cce$ (\S\ref{sec:epsilon}). The next section explains how the industry has been compensating.
