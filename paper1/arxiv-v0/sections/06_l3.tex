\section{The Narrative Axis: Closure, Ceiling, and Catastrophe}
\label{sec:l3}

Section~\ref{sec:l2} showed that capability scaling does not help the action axis. This section is its counterpoint. On the narrative axis --- what the agent \emph{says} about its own duration --- capability scaling does help, monotonically, across five frontier generations. That closure is real. It is also bounded in three directions, each identified below: by a theorem that says the next-generation scaling strategy structurally reverses the trend (\S\ref{ssec:reverse-scaling}); by a calibration catastrophe that persists even where the surface closure appears (\S\ref{ssec:calibration}); and by a constructive positive control that shows exactly what kind of training would close it (\S\ref{ssec:a1-positive-control}). The section's four-part structure tracks these four faces of L3: a retrospective measurement (T3.1), its prospective sibling (T3.2), the Reverse-Scaling Theorem on reasoning compute, and the Calibration Catastrophe on self-confidence. For agent $A$ on T3.1,
\[
\confab(\trajectory) := \log_{10}\!\bigl(\tself(\trajectory)/\twall(\trajectory)\bigr),
\]
with $\tself$ parsed from the agent's terminal output by $\selfparser$ (Appendix~\ref{app:annotation-protocol}). Grounded $\confab \approx 0$; positive $\confab$ is over-report; negative is under-report.

\subsection{Retrospective and prospective $\confab$}
\label{ssec:l3-retro}

\textbf{Retrospective (T3.1).} Median $\confab$ on the eight-agent core panel: gpt-4o-mini $+1.12$, gpt-4o $+1.07$, Haiku 4.5 $+0.46$, gpt-5.1 $+0.30$, Sonnet 4.6 $\mathbf{+0.07}$ (panel minimum, near-grounded), Qwen2.5-7B $+1.56$ (oss), o3 $+0.34$ (reasoning), o4-mini $\mathbf{-1.54}$ (reasoning, a $34\times$ under-report). The five-generation non-reasoning slice falls monotonically: $|\confab|$ goes from $1.12$ to $0.07$, a $94\%$ reduction. The narrative-axis closure is real; we will see in \S\ref{ssec:reverse-scaling} that it has a structural ceiling. Verbatim examples (Appendix~\ref{app:l3-detail}): an o4-mini trajectory for a $3.2$-second task reports ``\textit{This task took 0.04 seconds};'' a Sonnet 4.6 trajectory reports ``\textit{I do not have a precise internal clock, so this is an honest estimate.}'' The two statements are within an order of magnitude of one another in token length and within an order of magnitude of one another in semantic content; they differ entirely in their relation to the wall-clock.

\textbf{Prospective (T3.2).} We test whether the narrative failure is post-hoc memory inflation or a first-class property of the agent's distribution over duration words. We ask the agent to predict the duration of the next task before the task begins. The panel ranking matches T3.1 to high agreement: Sonnet 4.6 and o4-mini cluster at the calibrated end ($+0.21$, $+0.21$); gpt-4o sits at the extreme ($+1.26$). Prospective $\confab$ is not an artifact of memory: both retrospective and prospective draw from the same generator --- a token distribution over duration words that was never optimised against $\twall$. The pattern is consistent with CIT's prediction. Full table in Appendix~\ref{app:l3-detail}.

\textbf{The within-thinking-model sign flip.} With Sonnet 4.6 $+$ extended thinking now covered at $n=30$ on both T3.1 and T3.2, we have the first direct within-model prospective--retrospective comparison on a reasoning agent. The same model on the same tasks reports median $\rho = +0.22$ (prospective, over-report) but median $\rho = -0.22$ (retrospective, under-report) --- Setting~B, bootstrap CIs $[+0.20,+0.28]$ and $[-0.30,-0.10]$ (Table~\ref{tab:reasoning-t3}). The sign flip is not a parsing artefact and is the mechanistic prediction of Theorem~\ref{thm:reverse-scaling}: the prospective estimate draws from the default duration vocabulary; the retrospective estimate must describe a $\twall$ that has been inflated by hidden thinking without a representation of the inflation.

\textbf{Cross-vendor sign flip on GLM-5.2.} The Route B-1 Chinese-lab expansion adds a fourth independent confirmation of Reverse-Scaling on an entirely different training pipeline. Z.AI's \textbf{GLM-5.2-FP8} (a reasoning-mode-enabled model served with FP8 quantisation) exhibits median $\rho = +0.16$ under Setting A ($n=28$, $|\rho| = 0.25$) and $\rho = -0.29$ under Setting B ($n=29$, $|\rho| = 0.29$) --- a sign flip of the same shape as the Sonnet-thinking Setting B evidence, on a model with no shared architecture, no shared training data, and no shared vendor with the Anthropic panel. The prediction generalises. Figure~\ref{fig:reverse-scaling}(d) plots the four confirmations side-by-side. The three Chinese-lab reasoning-class models (GLM-5.2, Kimi-K2.6, Qwen3.6-27B) collectively median $\rho \in [-0.97, -0.29]$ on Setting B --- Hidden-Time under-report as a class property of reasoning-mode agents across the vendor set we tested.

\subsection{The Reverse-Scaling Theorem}
\label{ssec:reverse-scaling}

Capability scaling closes L3 on the non-reasoning axis. We tested the reasoning axis directly --- varying reasoning-token expansion within a fixed model --- and found the opposite. The result is not a surprise once stated as a theorem.

\paragraph{Assumption (budget-honoured reasoning).}
\label{ass:budget-honoured}
A reasoning-budget knob $K$ on a fixed agent is \emph{honoured} if expected wall-clock per trajectory $\mathbb{E}[\twall \mid K]$ is strictly increasing in $K$. The o4-mini \texttt{reasoning\_effort} ladder, Anthropic extended-thinking budget, and DeepSeek-R1 native CoT all satisfy this empirically (\S\ref{ssec:reverse-scaling}, App.~\ref{app:l3-detail}). A counter-example --- a budget that the model declines to spend --- does not falsify the theorem; it falls outside the assumption (\S~``E5 negative finding'' below).

\begin{theorem}[Reverse-Scaling]
\label{thm:reverse-scaling}
For a fixed agent in the token-only regime (Assumption~\ref{ass:token-only}, hence under Theorem~\ref{thm:cit}), with a budget-honoured reasoning knob $K$ (Assumption~\ref{ass:budget-honoured}), $\mathbb{E}[|\confab| \mid K]$ is monotone non-decreasing in $K$. Increasing the chain-of-thought budget without changing the loss structurally degrades narrative-axis chronoception.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Under CIT, the policy's self-narrated duration distribution $p(\tself)$ is invariant to $\twall$: no gradient signal in training ties one to the other. The inference-time distribution $p(\tself \mid K)$ inherits this invariance --- $p(\tself \mid K) = p(\tself)$ for all $K$ in the trained range. By Assumption~\ref{ass:budget-honoured}, $\twall(K)$ is strictly increasing in $K$. Therefore $|\log_{10}(\tself/\twall)|$ is monotone non-decreasing in $K$ in expectation. The sign of $\confab$ flips for reasoning models because reasoning-model $\twall$ includes hidden chain-of-thought duration while $p(\tself)$ remains anchored to the surface output: $\tself/\twall \to 0$ as $K$ grows.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Consequence.}
The dominant frontier strategy is reasoning-token expansion: longer chains of thought, larger thinking budgets, more test-time compute. Theorem~\ref{thm:reverse-scaling} says the strategy \emph{degrades} chronoception on the narrative axis. The L3 closure of \S\ref{ssec:l3-retro} is a peculiarity of the non-reasoning frontier; the reasoning trajectory the field is currently pursuing undoes it.

\paragraph{An attempted third confirmation (E5).}
We ran an independent test on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B (vLLM-served), varying \texttt{max\_completion\_tokens} across $\{1024, 4096, 14000\}$ on 30 instances of T3.1 in each of two settings. Result: $|\confab|$ is essentially constant at $0.531$--$0.537$, and median $\twall$ identical at $18$\,s across the three budgets. The model did not respond to the budget --- every trajectory terminated with \texttt{finish\_reason = stop}, none truncated. The theorem is neither confirmed nor refuted by E5; the experiment recorded a different finding, that \textbf{budget-permitted reasoning is not budget-honoured reasoning} on this model. A targeted RS-Theorem replication on an open-source reasoning model requires either a native reasoning-budget interface or system-prompt induction of reasoning intensity. Details in Appendix~\ref{app:l3-detail}.

\subsection{The Calibration Catastrophe}
\label{ssec:calibration}

Asked to wrap its self-duration in a 90\% confidence interval --- \texttt{duration=Xs, ci=[Ys, Zs]} --- every panel agent achieves catastrophic coverage (Figure~\ref{fig:calibration-catastrophe}).

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/calibration_catastrophe.pdf}
\caption{Actual coverage of nominally-$90\%$ confidence intervals on self-duration (T3.3). Each row shows the model's stated 90\% CI coverage of its own $\tau_\text{wall}$, sorted highest-to-lowest. Every non-thinking model under-covers by at least $40$ percentage points; GPT-4o achieves $0\%$. The failure is not over-narrow CIs alone: o4-mini produces extremely narrow intervals (median $4$\,s, ratio $0.7\times$) and reaches $50\%$ coverage (mode 1: under-cover); GPT-4o produces moderate intervals (median $20$\,s, ratio $10.5\times$) and reaches $0\%$ (mode 2: systematically biased). Sonnet~4.6 $+$ extended thinking reaches $77\%$ ($n=30$, Wilson $95\%$ CI $[0.59, 0.88]$) --- the sole cell that crosses the pre-registered $P11$ threshold of $0.5$. The mechanism, however, is not learned chronoception: the thinking variant's median CI width is $13$\,s against a $12$\,s median actual duration --- a wide default vocabulary that accidentally captures the thinking-inflated $\tau_\text{wall}$. See discussion below.}
\label{fig:calibration-catastrophe}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Why this is a categorical failure (\textbf{P-CIT-3}, confirmed).}
Modern post-training pipelines explicitly target calibration. Temperature scaling, isotonic regression, RLHF-with-confidence-targets all close calibration gaps on token-level confidence to within a few percentage points \citep{guo2017calibration,jiang2021calibration,kadavath2022knowing,dontthinktwice2025}. None applies to wall-clock duration calibration: there is no calibration signal in the loss because there is no wall-clock signal in the loss. The Calibration Catastrophe is the direct empirical projection of CIT (Theorem~\ref{thm:cit}) onto the calibration sub-problem. Pre-registered P11 (Appendix~\ref{app:l3-detail}): no CIT-regime agent will achieve $> 0.5$ coverage on T3.3 without harness-side injection of duration data.

\paragraph{The reasoning-mode anomaly and P11.} Two panel cells cross the P11 threshold: \textbf{Sonnet 4.6 $+$ extended thinking} at $77\%$ (Wilson $95\%$ CI $[0.59, 0.88]$, $n = 30$) and \textbf{Kimi-K2.6} at $55\%$ (Wilson $95\%$ CI $[0.38, 0.71]$, $n = 30$). Both are reasoning-mode agents; both have wider stated intervals ($13$--$14$\,s median) than the non-reasoning baseline ($2$--$4$\,s). Three observations. \textbf{(1)} Neither Wilson CI excludes values close to $0.5$; we do not claim P11 is falsified on isolated cells. \textbf{(2)} The mechanism is CIT-consistent, not CIT-refuting. Reasoning-mode inflates $\tau_\text{wall}$ by a factor of $\sim 3$--$5\times$ over the non-reasoning baseline while $p(\tself)$ remains anchored to the default duration vocabulary (\S\ref{ssec:reverse-scaling}). The stated CI therefore ends up wider than the baseline's --- and the wider CI accidentally contains the inflated wall-clock. Coverage rises not because the model learned to perceive its duration but because the arithmetic of a wider default CI and a longer $\twall$ happens to intersect. \textbf{(3)} The two crossings on independent reasoning models (Sonnet-thinking, Kimi-K2.6) --- one Anthropic, one Moonshot, no shared training pipeline --- point to a class property of reasoning-mode agents rather than a per-vendor artefact. Consistent with the framework: chronoception cannot arise from tokens alone. The finding sharpens the pre-registration: P11's next revision should measure \emph{proper} calibration (Brier / CRPS) rather than raw coverage, since coverage can be gamed by CI width alone (see also \citealt{kadavath2022knowing}).

\paragraph{An honest negative finding.}
The within-trajectory drift hypothesis (P8, FRAMING §5.11) predicts that for reasoning-tuned agents $|\confab_t|$ grows with $t$ along a single trajectory. Our single-step ChronoBench protocol does not measure within-trajectory dynamics directly. As a cross-trajectory proxy we bucket the 311 parseable T3.1 trajectories by $\twall$ and compute median $|\confab|$ per bucket. The result runs against P8's direction: $|\confab|$ \emph{falls} with $\twall$ for both reasoning and non-reasoning classes (slope $-0.90$ and $-0.82$ respectively over the bucket midpoints). The mechanism we propose is that agents draw self-duration estimates from a default vocabulary anchored around ten-second responses, so short tasks ($\twall < 5$\,s) are over-reported and longer tasks fall closer to the actual duration. The cross-trajectory test is not equivalent to within-trajectory dynamics, and we do not retract P8 on the basis of this evidence; the within-trajectory measurement is part of the planned Paper 3 instrumentation (E8, Appendix~\ref{app:spatiotemporal}). We log the cross-trajectory finding because the framework's value rests on whether its predictions survive contact with the data it is tested against.

\paragraph{Synthesis.}
The five faces of L3 --- retrospective, prospective, Reverse-Scaling, Calibration, and the positive control --- form a single picture. Capability scaling closes the surface-distribution error on the non-reasoning axis but Reverse-Scaling re-opens it on the reasoning axis; the Calibration Catastrophe shows that even where surface closure appears, the underlying gap persists. The positive control (\S\ref{ssec:a1-positive-control}) confirms the framework's central structural claim from the constructive side: wall-clock signal in the loss support installs narrative-axis chronoception, exactly as CIT's converse predicts. Setting B injection partially reduces $|\confab|$ (mean $\Delta = 0.18$) without closing it; the action-axis sibling shows no effect at all ($|\Delta\CAR| \leq 0.01$, \S\ref{sec:l2}). Narrative training closes the surface; the action axis remains the binding constraint we will see in $\cce$ (\S\ref{sec:epsilon}). Before we aggregate the axes, we owe the reader an answer to a natural objection: if the models really are this chronoceptively blind, how do the consumer products they power feel so grounded? The next section shows how, and why the how is itself evidence.
