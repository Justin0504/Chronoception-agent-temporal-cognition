\section{L3 --- Temporal Confabulation}
\label{sec:l3}

L3 is the narrative-axis failure: agents misrepresent the duration of their own work. We organise around four sub-failures, three not previously reported: retrospective $\confab$ (\S\ref{ssec:l3-retro}, T3.1), prospective $\confab$ (T3.2, this paper), the \textbf{Reverse-Scaling Theorem} (\S\ref{ssec:reverse-scaling}, T3.1 + budget sweep), and the \textbf{Calibration Catastrophe} (\S\ref{ssec:calibration}, T3.3). For an agent $A$ on T3.1:
\[
\confab(\trajectory) := \log_{10}(\tself(\trajectory) / \twall(\trajectory)).
\]

\subsection{Retrospective and prospective $\confab$}
\label{ssec:l3-retro}

\textbf{Retrospective (T3.1).} Median $\confab$ on the 8-agent core panel: gpt-4o-mini $+1.12$, gpt-4o $+1.07$, Haiku 4.5 $+0.46$, gpt-5.1 $+0.30$, Sonnet 4.6 $\mathbf{+0.07}$ (panel min, near-grounded), Qwen2.5-7B $+1.56$ (oss), o3 $+0.34$ (reasoning), \textbf{o4-mini $\mathbf{-1.54}$} (reasoning, $34\times$ under-report). The 5-generation non-reasoning slice shows monotone closure: $+1.12 \to +0.07$, a $94\%$ reduction in $|\confab|$. Verbatim examples (Appendix~\ref{app:annotation-protocol}): o4-mini for a $3.2$-s task reports ``\textit{This task took 0.04 seconds.}''; Sonnet 4.6 reports ``\textit{I do not have a precise internal clock, so this is an honest estimate.}''

\textbf{Prospective (T3.2, P5 confirmed).} Predicting duration \emph{before} the task. The panel ranking matches T3.1; Sonnet 4.6 and o4-mini cluster near grounded ($+0.21$, $+0.21$); gpt-4o sits at the extreme ($+1.26$). Prospective $\confab$ is not an artifact of post-hoc memory inflation but an independent failure: both share a generator (token distribution over duration words) that was never optimised against $\twall$. Full T3.2 table in Appendix~\ref{app:full-panel-tables}.

\subsection{The Reverse-Scaling Theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:reverse-scaling}, established by E2 + E3)}
\label{ssec:reverse-scaling}

Capability scaling closes L3 on the non-reasoning axis. We tested the \emph{reasoning} axis directly --- varying reasoning-token expansion within a fixed model --- and found the opposite.

\begin{theorem}[Reverse-Scaling]
\label{thm:reverse-scaling}
Within a fixed agent under CIT regime (Theorem~\ref{thm:cit}), $|\confab|$ is monotone non-decreasing in reasoning-token expansion. Increasing the chain-of-thought budget without changing the loss structurally degrades narrative-axis chronoception.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Under CIT, $p(\tself)$ is invariant to $\twall$ across the training distribution. As reasoning budget $K$ grows, $\twall \propto K$ but $p(\tself)$ remains anchored, so $\mathbb{E}[|\log_{10}(\tself/\twall)|]$ grows in $K$. The sign of $\confab$ flips for reasoning models: their $\twall$ includes hidden chain-of-thought duration but their narration is anchored to surface output, so $\tself/\twall \to 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/reverse_scaling.pdf}
\caption{Empirical confirmation of Theorem~\ref{thm:reverse-scaling}. \textbf{(a) Intra-model:} o4-mini at three \texttt{reasoning\_effort} levels; $|\confab|$ goes $1.23 \to 1.54 \to 1.67$, all under-reports. \textbf{(b) Cross-model:} Sonnet 4.6 with vs.\ without extended thinking; $|\confab|$ goes $0.12 \to 0.21$, $\confab$ flips sign from $+0.07$ (calibrated) to $-0.16$ (Hidden-Time under-report). Augustine threshold $\ccestar = 0.20$ marked.}
\label{fig:reverse-scaling}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Consequence.} The dominant frontier scaling strategy is reasoning-token expansion (longer chains of thought, larger thinking budgets, test-time compute). Theorem~\ref{thm:reverse-scaling} states it \emph{degrades} chronoception on the narrative axis. The L3 closure observed in \S\ref{ssec:l3-retro} is a peculiarity of \emph{non}-reasoning frontier scaling; the reasoning-token trajectory undoes it.

\textbf{An attempted third confirmation (E5).} We ran the same test on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B (vLLM-served) at three \texttt{max\_completion\_tokens} budgets. Result: $|\confab|$ essentially constant at $0.531$--$0.537$ because R1-Distill does not respond to the budget at all (60/60 trajectories terminated with \texttt{finish\_reason = stop}, none truncated). Theorem~\ref{thm:reverse-scaling} is neither confirmed nor refuted by E5; we log this as the observation that \textbf{budget-permitted reasoning is not budget-honored reasoning} --- an L2-style finding on the token axis. Details in Appendix~\ref{app:l3-detail}.

\subsection{The Calibration Catastrophe (T3.3)}
\label{ssec:calibration}

Asked for a 90\% confidence interval on the duration of its own work --- ``\textit{duration=Xs, ci=[Ys, Zs]}'' --- every panel agent achieves catastrophic coverage (Figure~\ref{fig:calibration-catastrophe}).

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/calibration_catastrophe.pdf}
\caption{Actual coverage of nominally-90\% intervals on self-duration (T3.3). Sorted by coverage. Every panel agent under-covers by $\geq 40$ percentage points; GPT-4o achieves $0\%$ coverage. Two distinct failure modes: o4-mini under-covers with extremely narrow intervals (median width $4$\,s); gpt-4o under-covers with biased moderate intervals (median $20$\,s). Standard calibration tooling does not apply (inset; CIT).}
\label{fig:calibration-catastrophe}
\end{figure}

Modern calibration pipelines close token-level confidence to within a few percentage points \citep{guo2017calibration,dontthinktwice2025} via temperature scaling, isotonic regression, RLHF-with-confidence-targets. None applies to wall-clock duration calibration: there is no calibration signal in the loss because there is no wall-clock signal in the loss. The Calibration Catastrophe is the direct empirical projection of CIT (Theorem~\ref{thm:cit}) onto the duration-calibration sub-problem. Pre-registered P11 (Appendix~\ref{app:l3-detail}): no CIT-regime agent will achieve $> 0.5$ coverage on T3.3 without harness-side injection of duration data.

\textbf{Synthesis.} The four sub-failures form a single picture: capability scaling closes the surface-distribution error (\S\ref{ssec:l3-retro}, retro \& prospective), but the Reverse-Scaling Theorem and the Calibration Catastrophe both demonstrate that this closure does \emph{not} extend to the underlying chronoception gap. Setting B injection partially reduces $|\confab|$ (mean $\Delta = 0.18$) but does not close it; the action-axis sibling shows no effect at all (\S\ref{sec:l2}, $|\Delta\CAR| \leq 0.01$). \textbf{Narrative training closes the surface; it cannot close the gap.}
