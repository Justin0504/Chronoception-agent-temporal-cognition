\section{The Injection Tell}
\label{sec:injection-tell}

A reader observing that frontier closed-source agents (ChatGPT, Claude.ai, Gemini app) answer ``what time is it now?'' correctly might suspect the Augustine Problem is overstated. The mechanism that produces those correct answers is, however, itself the strongest non-experimental evidence the problem is real. Closed-lab deployments do not give the agent chronoception --- they install external workarounds that route wall-clock information into the agent's token stream: (M1) system-prompt insertion of a date string; (M2) implicit \texttt{get\_current\_time()} tool calls; (M3) browser-tool side effects exposing timestamps. In each case the model performs no perception of time; the harness perceives time on its behalf and re-encodes the percept as tokens.

\textbf{Decisive evidence.} The universality of wall-clock injection across competing closed labs in the consumer product tier --- implemented independently, without coordination, by organisations with substantial commercial incentive to ship a cheaper alternative if it existed --- constitutes \textbf{decisive non-experimental evidence} that the underlying foundation models lack a representation of time. Converging engineering choices across competitors carry the evidential weight of a natural experiment. The within-Anthropic contrast is especially clean: the same Claude model accessed via Claude.ai web (injected, behaves chronoceptive) versus Anthropic API (not injected, refuses with training-cutoff disclosure). \textbf{The model is one; the chronoception is the harness's.}

\textbf{Setting B does not close the deeper failures.} Injection raises T1.1 pass rate to $\geq 96\%$ for every model but does not move T2.3 $\CAR$ (\S\ref{sec:l2}) and only partially reduces T3.1 $|\confab|$ (\S\ref{sec:l3}). Information at the prompt is not a representation; the action axis is untouched, and the narrative axis is only partially anchored.
