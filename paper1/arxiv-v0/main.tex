\begin{abstract}
Large language model agents are trained on token sequences but act in wall-clock time. We ask, empirically, whether they perceive their own time, and prove that under the standard suite of token-only training losses they cannot. We formalise a \emph{three-times} ontology ($\twall$, $\tstep$, $\tself$), define the \emph{Augustine Problem} as the failure of an agent's policy to enforce the identity linking them, and prove the \emph{Chronoception Impossibility Theorem} (CIT): token-only loss contains zero gradient signal aligning wall-clock duration with any internal representation. We introduce \textsc{ChronoBench}, a diagnostic benchmark over nine sub-capabilities across the three axes, and evaluate 10 frontier agents from 3 providers across roughly 4{,}000 trajectories. We report \textbf{five} primary findings: (1) every panel agent uses $\leq 5\%$ of wall-clock budgets given (L2 Step-Clock Conflation, unmoved by injection); (2) median retrospective $|\confab|$ closes monotonically with non-reasoning capability scaling ($+1.12 \to +0.07$ across 5 generations), while $\CAR$ does not; (3) we prove and empirically confirm the \emph{Reverse-Scaling Theorem}: reasoning-token expansion monotonically increases $|\confab|$ within fixed agents (o4-mini: $|\confab|$ goes $1.234 \to 1.537 \to 1.675$ across low/medium/high effort; Sonnet 4.6: $0.068 \to 0.156$ with extended thinking enabled, sign flipping from over- to under-report); (4) the \emph{Calibration Catastrophe}: every panel agent's nominally $90\%$ confidence interval on its own work duration achieves less than $50\%$ actual coverage, with gpt-4o achieving $0\%$; (5) consumer web-chat products inject today's date into system prompts at $100\%$ rate (verbatim leaked-system-prompt evidence), while developer-tool products do not. We close with the \emph{Agentic Timeline Hypothesis}: chronoception is the binding constraint on autonomous-agent deployment horizons, with pre-registered prediction P12 testable against METR HCAST and analogues. We release \textsc{ChronoBench}, the Injection Atlas, all 4{,}000 trajectory JSONs, and a \texttt{pip}-installable evaluation package.
\end{abstract}
