# Section 1 — Introduction (draft v0)

*Target length: ~1 page (≈600 words). Derives from `../FRAMING.md` §3, §3.1, §4, §5, §6, §9. To be folded into the final LaTeX position note in Phase 3.*

A frontier language-model agent, given a three-hour wall-clock budget for a task that requires five minutes, **does not use the three hours** — it terminates after four iterations of self-reflection within a few minutes. Asked retrospectively how long that took, it answers *"about an hour"* — over-reporting the duration of what was actually a five-minute job. Two failures appear on a single trajectory: the agent left the wall-clock budget unspent, and the agent misrepresented the duration of what it did. Garikaparthi (2026) documents this kind of duration over-reporting at $4\!-\!7\times$ prospective and order-of-magnitude error retrospective; Ma et al. (2026) document the budget under-use by showing that base Qwen3 reasoning length *"increases marginally under different time budgets."* These are not two quirks but two projections of a single structural defect — one that the current foundation-model paradigm cannot, in principle, repair from inside its training regime.

**Token-time and wall-clock time.** Modern language models are optimized under losses that are functionals of token sequences alone — pre-training cross-entropy, SFT, RLHF, RL with verifiable rewards, and reasoning supervision are all of this form. We call this the *token-time* regime. The wall-clock duration over which each token is generated is **not in the support** of any of these losses; it is invisible to the gradient. The deployment of foundation models as agents takes place in continuous physical time — the *wall-clock* regime — in which budgets are measured in seconds, deadlines arrive at fixed absolute moments, and consequences unfold without regard to step count. Wall-clock chronoception therefore cannot emerge from scaling data, parameters, or test-time compute; **it is in-principle excluded by the form of the loss**. The agentic turn has inherited a representational gap from the language-modeling era that no further scaling will close. Chronoception cannot be learned in token-time. It must be installed.

**The Augustine Problem.** We name this gap *the Augustine Problem*, after Augustine's *Confessions*: "if no one asks me, I know; if I want to explain it, I do not know." Foundation-model agents speak fluently about time — they propose schedules, estimate durations, accept deadlines — without representing it. The gap is *representational*, not *informational*: as we will show, providing the agent with a wall-clock timestamp as input does not, in itself, repair the failure, because the policy has no learned mechanism to translate the input into action selection or honest self-narration.

**The Injection Tell.** A reader might object that frontier closed-source agents (GPT-5.2, Gemini 2, Claude 4+) appear to answer "what time is it" correctly. They do — because their harnesses inject a wall-clock string into the system prompt or auto-invoke a `get_current_time` tool before the model's forward pass. The model itself performs no perception; the harness perceives time on its behalf and re-encodes the percept as tokens. The universality of this engineering workaround across competing closed labs — implemented independently, without coordination, by organizations with substantial commercial incentive to ship the cheaper alternative if it existed — constitutes *decisive non-experimental evidence* that the underlying foundation models lack a representation of time. We call this the *Injection Tell* and treat it as the strongest observational evidence available short of intervention.

**The framework.** We decompose the failure across three ontologically distinct projections of any agent trajectory — wall-clock time $\tau_{\text{wall}}$, step time $\tau_{\text{step}}$, and self-narrated time $\tau_{\text{self}}$ — and collapse the resulting three-axis failure into a single chronoceptive calibration error $\varepsilon$. We name three empirical laws, one per axis: *Agentic Parkinson's Law* (work inflates to fill the wall-clock budget; coefficient $\alpha$), *Step-Clock Conflation* (wall-clock budgets degrade into step-count terminators; ratio CAR), and *Temporal Confabulation* (self-reported durations exceed actual by 10–100×; ratio $\rho$). Reasoning-tuned models, the field's principal capability lever, exhibit *more* temporal confabulation than matched non-reasoning baselines, because reasoning expansion remains confined to token-time.

**Position.** We commit to a stronger claim than "a new evaluation axis is needed." We argue that $\varepsilon$ is *structurally* causally upstream of long-horizon agent failure: chronoceptive failure is observable on single-turn tasks where long-horizon outcomes are not yet defined, so the reverse causal direction is excluded by the construction of the framework. Closing the gap requires installation, not prompting — a wall-clock representation that is part of the agent's policy, not part of its input. We pre-register six falsifiable predictions and define an Augustine threshold $\varepsilon^* = 0.20$ as the qualifying line for chronoceptive groundedness; we predict that no foundation-model agent released as of 2026-05 satisfies it.

**Contributions.**

1. A formal framework for agent temporal cognition (§3), built on the in-principle insufficiency of token-loss training to induce wall-clock chronoception.
2. Three named empirical laws — Agentic Parkinson's Law, Step-Clock Conflation, Temporal Confabulation — with quantitative metrics ($\alpha$, CAR, $\rho$), one per axis, unified by a regime transition $B^{*}$ and strengthened, on the $\tau_{\text{self}}$ axis, into a Reverse-Scaling Theorem.
3. A single scalar $\varepsilon$ aggregating chronoceptive failure, and a model invariant $N_{A}$ characterizing chronoceptive blindness in one number per agent.
4. The Chronoception Upstream Hypothesis as a *structural* causal claim, grounded in the single-turn observability of $\varepsilon$; the Augustine threshold $\varepsilon^{*} = 0.20$ as the qualifying line for chronoceptive groundedness.
5. The Injection Tell as decisive non-experimental evidence: closed-lab wall-clock injection is read as direct support for the structural diagnosis, and the two-armed prediction P1a / P1b makes that reading falsifiable.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. §2 develops the token-time / wall-clock impedance diagnosis. §3 defines the three times. §4 states the three laws and their structural symmetry. §5 introduces the Chronoception Upstream Hypothesis. §6 presents pilot evidence under both no-injection (Setting A) and with-injection (Setting B) regimes. §7 records four pre-registered predictions. §8 develops implications for evaluation, training, and agency. §9 reviews related work. §10 concludes.
