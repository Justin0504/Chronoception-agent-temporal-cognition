\begin{abstract}
Paper~1 (\emph{\Augustine}) proved that LLM agents cannot acquire wall-clock
\chrono{} from any token-only training loss (the Chronoception Impossibility
Theorem, CIT) and that reasoning-token expansion degrades it (the
\ReverseScaling). If \chrono{} cannot be \emph{trained in}, it must be
\emph{installed}. \ChronoStack{} enumerates four installation routes ---
loss extension, tool interface, harness scaffolding, and an architectural time
primitive --- and measures, on \ChronoBench, how much of the gap each one closes
and where each one fails. We find that \chrono{} is installable, but that
\textbf{the install must reach where the agent's wall-clock actually is}, and
this differs by axis and by model class. A wall-clock-supported loss (route~1)
and a clock tool (route~2) close the narrative axis (self-reported duration,
$|\confab|$) for non-reasoning models --- a clock tool drops median $|\confab|$
from $1.06$ to $0.17$ and is used spontaneously. But for reasoning models a
pulled clock fails ($|\confab|$ stays $0.81$, $0\%$ grounded) because it times
only the visible action stream while the wall-clock is dominated by hidden
reasoning --- the Hidden-Time signature of Paper~1. The fix is to bracket the
\emph{entire} invocation: a harness-computed elapsed-time signal grounds the same
reasoning model to $|\confab|=0.17$, $86\%$ grounded. On the action axis, a live
scaffolding clock eliminates deadline overruns across models and a $6\times$
budget range, where Paper~1 showed static date injection moves nothing. The
architectural primitive builds the bracketing in natively and is pre-registered.
We release all routes, code, trajectories, and a unifying
route~$\times$~axis~$\times$~model-class map.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction --- from impossibility to installation}

Paper~1 established the negative result: token-only losses carry no wall-clock
gradient (CIT), so additional scale, longer reasoning, and prompt-level date
injection cannot close the \chrono{} gap. The constructive question follows
immediately: what \emph{can}? \ChronoStack{} is the catalogue of answers and the
measurement of each.

The organising claim of this paper: time is installable, but an install only
works if its signal reaches the part of the trajectory where the agent's
wall-clock is actually spent. Two structural facts from Paper~1 shape every
route: (i)~the \emph{narrative axis} ($\tself$, $|\confab|$) is text-trainable
while the \emph{action axis} ($\tstep/\twall$, $\CAR$) is not; (ii)~for
\emph{reasoning models} the wall-clock hides inside chain-of-thought the surface
stream cannot see.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Route 1 --- loss extension (A.1 toy positive control)}
Wall-clock-supported LoRA SFT on T3.1. Qwen2.5-1.5B: median $|\confab|$
$1.37 \to 0.30$ (crosses the \AugustineThreshold{} $\ccestar=0.20$ on T3.1
score, $0.69 \to 0.15$); Qwen2.5-7B: $0.93 \to 0.51$ (partial). This demonstrates
CIT's converse: putting wall-clock in the loss support installs narrative-axis
\chrono{}. Cost: GPU.

\subsection{Route 2 --- tool interface, and its reasoning-model failure}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\caption{Tool interface on T3.1 ($n=30$ per condition).}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Model & no-tool $|\confab|$ & tool $|\confab|$ & used $\geq 2\times$ \\
\midrule
gpt-4o-mini & 1.06 & \textbf{0.17} & $97\%$ (spontaneous) \\
o4-mini & 1.37 & \textbf{0.86} & $100\%$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

A clock tool grounds the \emph{non-reasoning} model and is adopted \emph{without
instruction} (the bare tool and prompted-tool conditions are indistinguishable).
The \emph{reasoning} model calls the clock twice in $100\%$ of trajectories and
faithfully reports the measured span --- yet $|\confab|$ stays $0.86$, because the
span ($2$--$7$\,s) is ${\sim}7\times$ smaller than the true wall-clock
($8$--$23$\,s): the reasoning happens in hidden tokens \emph{outside} the window
between the two visible calls. The agent uses the tool perfectly and is still
wrong.

\subsection{Route 2 $\to$ 4 bridge --- bracket the whole invocation}
The diagnosis predicts a fix: compute elapsed time in the \emph{harness}, from
the invocation start, so it includes hidden reasoning. Same T3.1, adding a
\texttt{get\_elapsed\_time()} whose value the harness returns.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\caption{Naive clock vs.\ harness-bracketed elapsed tool ($n=30$). Grounded
$= |\confab| < 0.3$.}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Model & clock\_tool $|\confab|$ (grounded) & elapsed\_tool $|\confab|$ (grounded) \\
\midrule
gpt-4o-mini & 0.20 ($62\%$) & 0.13 ($67\%$) \\
o4-mini & \textbf{0.81 ($0\%$)} & \textbf{0.17 ($86\%$)} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Bracketing collapses the reasoning model's error from $0.81$ to $0.17$ and
grounds $86\%$ of trajectories --- same agent, same task, only the timing signal
changed. For the non-reasoning model the two tools are equivalent (no hidden time
to recover). \textbf{The route-2 failure was the signal's visibility, not the
agent's willingness.}

\subsection{Route 3 --- scaffolding (budget-honoring loop)}
A multi-step loop where the agent decides when to stop, given a wall-clock budget
$\budget$, with vs.\ without a live \texttt{[clock] elapsed/remaining/step}
readout. Sweep $\budget \in \{20,60,120\}$\,s $\times$ \{gpt-4o-mini, o4-mini\},
$n=10$.

Native (no-clock) behaviour is the opposite of the naive L2 expectation: agents
\emph{overrun} deadlines, worsening with budget (gpt-4o-mini: $50/100/80\%$ of
runs over budget at $\budget=20/60/120$). The live scaffold drives the overrun
rate to ${\sim}0$ across both models and every budget (gpt-4o-mini
$50/100/80\% \to 0/0/0$; o4-mini $100/30/0\% \to 10/0/0$) --- the action-axis win
static date injection could not buy. Limits: the scaffold trades overrun for mild
under-use (on-clock median $\CAR$ $0.65$--$0.83$), and a reasoning model
under-uses large budgets regardless ($\CAR \approx 0.29$ at $\budget=120$ either
way).

\subsection{Route 4 --- architectural primitive}
Wall-clock as a native learned input. Interface and feature encoder implemented
(\texttt{chronoception/stack/time\_channel.py}): per-step features
$(\text{elapsed\_s},\,\text{delta\_s},\,\text{step\_index},\,\text{remaining\_frac})$
anchored to the harness clock around the entire invocation --- covering exactly
the hidden-time blind spot route~2 hits. No training-free empirical MVP;
experiment E-ARCH (LoRA time-token on the A.1 harness, $\cce$ vs.\ routes 2/3
baselines) is pre-registered. Needs GPU.

\section{Synthesis --- route $\times$ axis $\times$ model class}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\caption{Which route installs what, by axis and model class.}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
 & narrative axis ($|\confab|$) & action axis ($\CAR$ / overrun) \\
\midrule
non-reasoning & route 1 (train); route 2 clock (spontaneous) & route 3 scaffold (overruns $\to 0$) \\
reasoning & route 2 clock fails (Hidden-Time) $\to$ bracketed / route 4 & route 3 scaffold (overruns); under-use residue \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Three regularities. \textbf{(1) Installable, but axis-specific.} The narrative
axis yields to loss or tool; the action axis yields to a live pushed clock, not
to information alone. \textbf{(2) Reasoning models need the signal to bracket
hidden time.} A pulled clock sees only the surface stream and fails; a
harness/architectural signal that brackets the whole invocation succeeds
($0.81 \to 0.17$). \textbf{(3) Push beats pull for the action axis.} Scaffolding
(push) eliminates overruns where the tool (pull) and static injection do not act.

\section{Limitations and honest negatives}
Routes 1 and 4 require training/GPU; route~4 has no empirical result yet
(interface and pre-registration only). Scaffolding's median-$\CAR$ effect is null
at budgets near the natural landing point; the real signal is dispersion/overrun
(pre-registered as primary in the sweep), and reasoning-model under-use at large
budgets is unsolved by scaffolding. Tool/bridge use a local duration-parse
fallback for the ``Task duration: $X$\,s'' phrasing; the canonical parser is
tried first for comparability. Single seed, modest $n$ per cell; provider-side
tool and temperature behaviour may shift across model versions.

\section{Conclusion}
\Chrono{} can be installed, and which route works is predicted by \emph{which
axis} and \emph{which model class} you target. The programme's sharpest result is
the reasoning-model fix: the failure of a clock tool is not the agent's --- it is
the signal's blindness to hidden reasoning, and bracketing the whole invocation
repairs it. That principle, proven here with a cheap tool proxy, is what the
architectural primitive (route~4) is designed to make native.
